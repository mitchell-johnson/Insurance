# Underwriting and new business

`crates/underwriting` (`ic-underwriting`) takes a proposal from quote to policy: the disclosure, the rules, the people who decide what the rules cannot, the offer, and the one command that issues the policy in the core. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md`. Its shipped configuration is in `products/underwriting/` and its authority policy in `config/cedar/underwriting.cedar`.

## What it does

- **Configuration as code, activated through the log** (`config.rs`, `shipped.rs`). Exclusion wordings, reason codes, question sets, rule sets, authority profiles and settings are Starlark files loaded into typed values. Each is activated by a command that names an author, two other approvers and a change reference. Versions only go up, a code list never loses a code, a changed rule set needs its backtest digest, and nobody approves their own profile. Everything is looked up by date, so a proposal is judged by what was in force when it was signed.
- **Two disclosure regimes.** Question set 1 is the duty of disclosure; set 2 is the reasonable-care duty from 15 November 2027. The regime a life answered under is recorded on the file and travels with every later non-disclosure review.
- **No clinical content in the log.** Answers are sealed in the clinical store (`clinical.rs`): AES-256-GCM under a key per person, that key wrapped by a key that never leaves Azure Key Vault, the record's reference bound into the seal, every read given a purpose and recorded. Destroying a person's key ends access to all their records, in backups too, and an erasure marker stops anything more being stored about them. Commands carry hashes, vault references and the per-cover assessment that `prepare_disclosure` works out from the answers before the command is proposed. Rules are named by opaque codes (`UR-0001`, with what each is about kept in the rule set), because the codes of the rules that fired travel with each disclosure; the rule set loader refuses any other kind of id (`tests/lifecycle.rs::the_log_of_an_underwriting_run_holds_ids_and_references_only`).
- **Signed steps** (`signing.rs`). Disclosure, a tele-interview confirmation, an offer response, a consent and a re-quote are signed on the applicant's device with ed25519 over a message that names the step, so a signature for one step cannot be replayed as another.
- **Routing** (`flow.rs`). The worst disposition across a life's covers decides the path: decline or defer goes to a person for review, a referral opens a case in the right queue, a tele-interview is scheduled, evidence is requested only with the applicant's consent to obtain records, and clean or rule-rated answers are decided by the rules unless the sum insured is over the straight-through limit.
- **Capacity before terms.** Every accepted cover reserves capacity with reinsurance before a decision takes effect. An automatic answer lets it proceed, a facultative answer waits for the reinsurer's terms and applies them, and an excluded answer returns the case to a person. A reservation is released whenever the cover it was for is no longer wanted and confirmed when the policy is issued.
- **Case work under authority** (`casework.rs`, `authority.rs`). Underwriters take, hand over and move cases, request evidence, ask a medical officer's opinion, and propose terms. A Cedar policy and the underwriter's profile decide what each may do: sum insured, loading and exclusion limits by grade, no work on a file where they declared a conflict, no countersigning their own decision. A decline, a deferral, a sum over the countersign limit or a loading over 100% needs a second signature from someone senior who has not touched the file.
- **Offers** (`intake.rs`). Standard terms are accepted at once. Anything else is an offer with an expiry that the applicant accepts, declines or asks to have looked at again. Where some cover is declined or deferred and the rest is offered, the person is also sent a decline notice of its own, with the reasons they may be told (the letter template `uw-declined` carries the route to ask for review; its wording is not yet written). When one of two joint lives drops out, the other is asked again before a single-life policy is issued, because their earlier acceptance was of a different contract.
- **Issue.** When every life is final the module asks the core to issue the policy with one command bound to the quoted product version. On confirmation it confirms the capacity, sends the rating factors to reinsurance and ends any interim cover. A refusal from the core is kept with its reason and can be retried.
- **Interim accidental death cover**, where configured, is requested when the proposal opens, capped, and ended when the proposal ends in any way.
- **The clock** (`external.rs`). One `Sweep` command per day does bounded work in ID order: lapses proposals past their expiry, chases evidence and marks it unobtainable after the configured chasers, escalates cases nobody has touched, and expires offers.
- **Existing cover from the life index (addendum entry 2).** With the applicant's consent, the existing-cover question is pre-filled from the life index across partitions. Without consent nothing is read. What was read, and that it was pre-filled, is recorded on the file, and the applicant's own answer is still theirs to give.
- **A scheme member's consent to itemising.** On a proposal for scheme excess cover the member may agree, by a signed step, that the employer sees a loaded premium (`ConsentKind::EmployerSeesLoading`). The accepted terms carry it to group administration as `employer_may_see_loading`; without it the answer never claims consent.
- **Adviser licence loss (addendum entry 4).** When an adviser's licence is suspended or lost, their access to the file ends at once and the applicant is told. The proposal continues without them.
- **Delegated underwriting** (`special.rs`). A delegate's decisions come in with the delegate's own reference, are checked against the delegated authority (products, sums, loadings, dates, tolerance for late reporting, and codes in our own lists) and, where outside it, nothing is bound: the reason is returned and the life goes to our medical queue. A repeated or missing reference is refused. The delegate's file can be called for and its arrival recorded.
- **Migrated policies.** Terms on a migrated policy are carried as data from the old system. Nothing is defaulted.
- **After issue.** Claims asks for the terms on a cover, the underwriters who touched a life, and the file to be opened for a claim. A non-disclosure review needs seniors who never touched the file and two of them for a remedy. Peer review draws a sample of decisions for a senior who had no part in them.
- **Refusals leave no trace.** A refused command changes nothing. A repeated command gets its first result, marked as a repeat.

## How it talks to the rest

In: commands in an `Envelope` (`key`, `caller`, `at`, `today`, `command`). Callers are fixed names: `config`, `new-business`, `migration`, `adviser-portal`, `evidence`, `underwriting-console`, `reinsurance`, `issuer`, `timer`, `adviser-licensing`, `claims`, `delegate-api`, and `customer-app`, which may only record or withdraw the life's own consents (`record_consent`), signed where the kind needs it.

Out, on each applied command: events for the audit trail, and `Effect`s, each with a stable key that is its idempotency key at the receiver. Effects are for reinsurance (reserve, release and confirm capacity, record risk factors), the core (issue the policy, issue and end interim cover, apply terms, a non-disclosure remedy), evidence providers (request, chase, cancel), a delegate (decide a member's excess, send the file) and people (notices).

The reply to a command carries its effects, and the same effects wait, numbered, in a replicated outbox until the driver acknowledges them (`Query::Outbox`, `Command::AcknowledgeEffects` from caller `effects-driver`), so a lost reply loses nothing. An effect that cannot be delivered is parked with its reason (`Command::ParkEffect`, `Query::Parked`) and a person with an authority profile puts it back (`Command::RequeueEffect` from `underwriting-console`).

Queries (`special::Query`, answered by `special::answer`): a proposal, the terms on a policy's cover, the underwriters of a life, a queue with ages, an underwriter's workload, a report over a period, the exclusion library on a date, and the outbox.

For claims: `codes::ExclusionLibrary::matches` decides whether an exclusion applies to a coded cause, and `codes::CAUSE_ROOTS` lists the roots cause codes hang from. Claims owns the coding of a cause; this module owns the wordings and the match. Claims lives inside the core and cannot depend on this crate, so `ExclusionLibrary::for_claims()` produces the same library in the form the claims rules are activated with, and a test holds the two matchers to the same answer for every shipped code.

Risk codes for reinsurance: a capacity request carries `risk_codes`, the pursuit, occupation and travel tags (`pursuit.*`, `occupation.*`, `travel.*`) that the cover's latest disclosure declared, plus the cause codes of exclusions applied to that cover when they are in those families. A question tags its answers in the question set (`yes_no(..., risk_tag=...)`, `choice(..., risk_tags={...})`); the loader refuses a tag outside the three families and any tag on a clinical question, so health data is never sent. The shipped tags are `pursuit.unspecified`, `pursuit.aviation`, `pursuit.motorsport`, `pursuit.diving`, `pursuit.climbing`, `pursuit.other`, `occupation.hazardous`, `travel.high_risk` and `travel.lived_overseas`. Reinsurance matches them against treaty exclusions, so the strings are kept stable; a new one is added, never renamed.

Hosting: `ic_underwriting::module_spec()` is in `ic_node::modules()`, so every ledger node hosts the module under the name `underwriting`. It starts empty and takes its configuration through the log.

## The driver

`ic-underwriting` (`driver.rs`, `service.rs`, `deploy/k8s/underwriting.yaml`) is the process that carries the outbox. It holds no state and two run at once: every delivery has a key taken from the effect, so each receiver applies a repeat once.

| Effect | Goes to | Answer brought back |
|---|---|---|
| Reserve capacity | Reinsurance module, key `uw:{reservation}:reserve`, then its `capacity` question | `CapacityAnswered`, key `cap:{reservation}` |
| Release and confirm capacity, rating factors | Reinsurance module | none |
| Issue the policy | Core, `IssueQuoted` from `new-business`, which refuses with `product_changed` when the product version is not the one quoted | `PolicyIssued` with the layers the core wrote, or `IssueRejected` |
| Terms for an increase on a policy in force | Core, `AddUnderwrittenLayer` from `underwriting`, one a cover, key `{proposal}/increase/{life}/{cover}`: a new layer from the business date, with the days left of a billed period billed as an adjustment | `PolicyIssued` with the layers, or `IssueRejected` |
| Issue and end interim cover | Core, `IssuePolicy` and `CancelPolicy` | `InterimIssued` |
| Terms or an ended proposal for a group excess case | Group module, `record_excess_decision`, key `uw:{effect key}` | none |
| A scheme member's loading moved to them, or the offer to move it ended | Group module, `record_excess_decision`: the terms again with `member_pays_loading` under the new issue key, or `reoffer_declined` under `uw:{proposal}/reoffer/{n}/ended` | none |
| Notices for an underwriter, a queue lead or the underwriting manager | The `underwriting-notices` event hub (`notices.rs`), ID `underwriting-notice:{outbox number}`, properties `event-id` and `kind`; the body names the notice, who it is for, the proposal and the life, never the reasons | none |
| Letters to a customer or an adviser | Documents module, `request_letter`, request and key `uw:{outbox number}` | delivered, or parked with documents' refusal |
| Evidence orders (request, chase, cancel) | The `underwriting-evidence` event hub, ID `underwriting-order:{outbox number}`, body `{order, request, kind?, party?}`; the provider's adapter reads it | none; answers come back as `evidence_acknowledged`, `evidence_received`, `evidence_unobtainable` |
| Requests for a delegate's file | The `underwriting-delegates` event hub, same ID scheme, body `{proposal, life, delegate_ref}` | none; the file comes back as `delegate_file_received` |
| Terms for an alteration on a policy in force | Core, `AlterUnderwrittenTerms` from `underwriting`, one a cover, key `{proposal}/alteration/{life}/{cover}`: each layer of the life's cover on other terms ends and is written again from the business date with the same sum insured, origin and rating date; the days left of a billed period are billed or credited as an adjustment. A cover already on the terms (`no_change`) is nothing to write | `PolicyIssued` with the new layers, or `IssueRejected` |
| Terms for a reinstatement | Core, `ApproveReinstatement` from `underwriting`, key `{proposal}/reinstatement`: the decision and any terms that differ are recorded against the lapse, good until the proposal expires. `Reinstate` (collections or policy servicing, once the arrears are paid) needs it when the lapse has run past the product's `reinstatement_evidence_free_days`, and puts the cover on the terms from that day | `PolicyIssued` with no layers, or `IssueRejected` |
| A non-disclosure remedy | Core, `AvoidPolicy` or `VaryForNonDisclosure` from `underwriting`, key `{review}/remedy`, the review as the case; the core does nothing twice for a case | delivered, or parked with the core's refusal |

Proposal progress for the portals (`progress.rs`): once the outbox is empty, the driver reads `Query::Progress`, publishes each waiting proposal's rows to the channel module (`publish_rows`, actor module `underwriting`, key `{proposal}/progress/{version}`), and tells the module with `ProgressPublished`. Each proposal has a row `{policy}/underwriting/{proposal}` of kind `proposal` that anyone on the policy may see: entry, status (`open`, `issued`, `lapsed`, `withdrawn`, or `ended` for a decline, since a decline implies health), whose move it is (`applicant` or `insurer`), received and expiry dates. Each life has a row `{policy}/underwriting/{proposal}/{life}` of kind `proposal_life`, marked sensitive with the life as subject, so only that person sees their stage. A refusal by the channel module is logged and counted and the version let go.

A receiver's refusal that means "already done" or "no longer wanted" is counted as stale and acknowledged. Any other refusal parks the effect. Only a receiver that cannot be reached stops a pass, and the next pass starts from the same effect. Group administration's own driver reads the same outbox without acknowledging and sends the same excess decisions under the same key, so either may arrive first.

Once a day the driver sends `Sweep` as caller `timer`, a page at a time, under keys of the day and the page start. After the sweep it asks the collections module for `reinstatements_for_underwriting` (the lapsed policies whose reinstatement the core refused with `underwriting_required`), reads each policy from the core (on the partition collections names, or by asking each partition), and opens `reinstatement_proposal` for it as caller `collections` under key `collections:{proposal}`. A policy already opened is not opened again; one no longer lapsed is reported in the sweep's result and skipped; and when collections cannot be asked, the sweep still stands and the next day's tries again.

Settings: `IC_NODES` and `IC_EVENT_HUBS_ENDPOINT` (required), the cluster token as for every worker, the workers' Azure identity, `IC_UNDERWRITING_EVENT_HUB` (`underwriting-notices`), `IC_UNDERWRITING_CYCLE_MS` (2000), `IC_UNDERWRITING_PATIENCE_MS` (10000), `IC_UNDERWRITING_BATCH` (100), `IC_UNDERWRITING_SWEEP_PAGE` (200).

## Takeover terms and reinsurance's reference on a scheme member's excess

Group administration's `group_excess` entry can carry `takeover_accepted_minor`, the excess a previous insurer had accepted that the scheme's takeover terms honour without evidence, and `reinsurance_reference`, when reinsurance asked for the member's cover to be underwritten because of their other cover. An excess no larger than the takeover amount is decided when the proposal opens: standard terms, reason `RC-0975`, no disclosure, and then reinsurance capacity as for any decision. A larger excess is underwritten in full, because the honoured amount is all that was promised. When reinsurance gave a reference the cover is always underwritten, since the treaty has not agreed the risk; the reference is kept on the proposal and goes back to reinsurance as `excess_reference` on the capacity request, where its `excess_underwriting` question shows the case is with underwriting (`tests/takeover.rs`).

## Moving a loading to a scheme member

A scheme's terms can bill a member's loading to the employer in one total only while enough members hold underwritten cover (`docs/group.md`). When the count falls below that, staff can ask for the loading to move: `reoffer_excess_terms {proposal, reason}` from caller `group-admin`, on an issued `group_excess` proposal whose terms carry a loading. The member was told the employer pays, so nothing moves until they agree. The member is sent an offer letter with no reasons, and answers through `respond_to_offer` with the offer `{proposal}/reoffer/{n}`, signed on their device like any offer; they can accept or decline but not counter. Accepted, the same terms are applied again under `{proposal}/issue/{n}` with `member_pays_loading`, and every later issue of the proposal says so. When the core has written the replacement layer, the proposal is issued again with no new capacity confirmation, risk factors or issue letter, because the cover itself did not change. Declined, or not answered within the offer days, the terms in place stand and group administration is told (`reoffer_declined`); staff may ask again. The sweep reaches an issued proposal only while such an offer is open (`src/reoffer.rs`, `tests/reoffer.rs`).

## The disclosure intake

`ic-underwriting-intake` (`disclosures.rs`, `intake_service.rs`, `deploy/k8s/underwriting-intake.yaml`) is the only writer of disclosures. It runs under the clinical identity and talks to the module through the ledger nodes.

1. `POST /v1/disclosures/to-sign` with `{proposal, life, step, answers, adviser_entered}` (`step` is `disclosure` or `interview`): the answers are checked against the question set in force for the proposal (`Query::DisclosureBasis`) and the rules run; the answer is the record's hash and the exact bytes the life signs (`ic.underwriting.{step}.v1`, proposal, life, hash). Nothing is stored. Answers that do not fit are a 422 listing the problems.
2. `POST /v1/disclosures` with the same answers and `signature` (the device key's `SignedStep`: `key_id`, `public_key`, `signature`, and `alg`, either `ed25519` (the default) or `ecdsa-p256` for the keys the channel identity module enrols, with the key as its point or SubjectPublicKeyInfo and the signature in ASN.1 or fixed form): the answers are prepared again, giving the same hash; the signature is checked before anything is written; the record is sealed under the life's own key as `clinical://{party}/{step}/{hash}`; and `SubmitDisclosure` (or `ConfirmInterview`) goes to the module as caller `new-business` under key `{proposal}/{life}/{step}/{hash}`. A repeat finds the record and the key already there and is answered as a duplicate.

Callers need an Entra token for `IC_UNDERWRITING_INTAKE_AUDIENCE` with the app role for the route: `new-business` (disclosures), `underwriting-console` (reading them back), `evidence` (provider reports) or `delegate-api` (delegate files). The portal holds the first two. Settings: `IC_NODES`, the cluster token, `IC_ENTRA_TENANT_ID`, `IC_UNDERWRITING_INTAKE_AUDIENCE`, `IC_CLINICAL_BLOB_ENDPOINT`, `IC_CLINICAL_KEY_VAULT_URL`, `IC_CLINICAL_WRAPPING_KEY` (required); `IC_CLINICAL_CONTAINER` (`clinical`), `IC_ENTRA_AUTHORITY`, `IC_UNDERWRITING_INTAKE_LISTEN` (`0.0.0.0:8080`).

## Evidence and delegate files coming back

The intake service also takes what comes back from outside (`src/inbound.rs`). `POST /v1/evidence` (app role `evidence`) takes `{"outcome": "acknowledged", request, provider_ref}`, `{"outcome": "received", request, report, cost_minor}` with the report in base64, or `{"outcome": "unobtainable", request}`. A report is sealed in the clinical store under the person's party (`clinical://{party}/evidence/{sha-256}`) before the module is told where it is; the command keys are `evidence:{request}:{outcome}`, so a retry files nothing twice. `POST /v1/delegate-files` (app role `delegate-api`) takes `{proposal, life, file}`, seals it the same way, and sends `delegate_file_received` with its hash; the module takes it only for a life a delegate decided. Documents are at most 20 MB. Unknown requests or proposals are 404, a document that is not base64 or is empty or too large is 422, and a module refusal is 409. The adapters that read the evidence and delegates hubs and call these routes are the providers' and delegates' integrations, which are not built here; the network policy admits pods labelled `ic-evidence-adapter` and `ic-delegate-gateway`.

## A replacement with a top-up

When a customer's new adviser replaces their cover and adds to it, the application is the increase alone: `open_proposal` with entry `increase` on the policy in force, an adviser, and `replacement: true` (refused `replacement` otherwise). The cover in force carries on untouched, so there is no gap and no new waiting period on the amount replaced, and the increase becomes a second layer on the same cover: one cover, one sum insured. Before a life's disclosure is taken, the life confirms the replacement disclosure with a signed step (`record_consent`, kind `replacement_disclosure`, signed over `consent/{proposal}/{life}/replacement_disclosure`); a disclosure sent first is refused `replacement_disclosure`, and once the disclosure is taken the confirmation cannot be withdrawn (`too_late`). The disclosure itself (what is kept and gained, the premiums compared, and that the adviser earns nothing on the amount replaced) is shown by the adviser portal; its wording is not yet written. Moving the replaced cover to a newer product version is a servicing change in the core and is not built (`tests/replacement.rs`, and the driver test `a_replacement_with_a_top_up_underwrites_only_the_increase_on_the_same_cover`).

The life's progress row on a replacement carries `replacement: {cover, sum_insured_minor, premium_minor}` (the cover the new layer goes on, its sum insured, the quoted premium), which the portal reads for the disclosure statement `uw-replacement-disclosure` (agreed with the portal thread, 14 Nov 2026).

## A leaver continuing cover above the automatic limit

Decided by Mitchell on 13 November 2026 (in the group thread): a scheme may let a leaver continue their underwritten cover above the automatic limit; it is a scheme setting, off by default. Agreed with group administration the same day. Group issues the continuation policy itself for the automatic amount (only it may set `continuation_of`), then opens `open_proposal` as `group-admin`, ID `grp-cont:{scheme}:{offer}`, channel `group-continuation`, one life and one cover for the amount above automatic, entry `{entry: "group_continuation", policy, scheme_policy, scheme_life, inception, earlier}`, where `policy` is the continuation policy and `earlier` names the member's `grp-uw:` excess proposals in place when they left. The open is refused `continuation` unless every earlier proposal is issued scheme excess cover on the same party, of the same kind of cover (scheme life counts as life), and together they accepted at least the amount continued, or if the inception is after the day it is taken up. The amount is decided as the proposal opens, with no disclosure, no evidence and no offer, on the earlier terms (the heaviest loading and every exclusion where they differ, the exclusions from the inception), source `continuation`, reason RC-0976. Capacity is reserved with reinsurance as for any retail cover. The driver writes it as `AddUnderwrittenLayer` on the continuation policy, and group reads `proposal_ended` (entry `group_continuation`, outcome `issued`, or `withdrawn`, `lapsed`, `declined`) from the outbox. The layer starts on the inception: the driver sends it as `UnderwrittenLayer.start`, so one taken up after its inception is backdated to it and the core bills the days already passed (`src/continuation.rs`, `tests/continuation.rs`, and the driver test `a_continued_excess_becomes_a_layer_on_the_continuation_policy`).

## Product conditions on who may apply (AIA products)

Built 14 November 2026 for the AIA products (docs/aia-products.md). A new-business application is checked against its product's conditions before anything else (`src/eligibility.rs`), and one outside them is refused `eligibility` with the reason, so the adviser corrects it and no decision is recorded on the life. The checks are business settings (`Settings.eligibility`), activated with the settings and their approvals, and ship as: TPD, income and accident cover on AIA Living, Living Business, Start-Up Income and Starter need the life's occupation class; TPD is taken to 55, or 60 in class 1; the income benefit period to 70 only in classes 1 and 2; Family Protection to an age runs at least 15 years from entry; a child under 16 has at most $250,000 of trauma on AIA Living and Living Business; Essentials Life takes at most $500,000 from 61 to 70. The class lists (class 1 as clerical, managerial and professional; classes 1 and 2 as accepted for income to 70) were confirmed by Mitchell on 14 November 2026; they change by a settings activation if AIA's occupation guide maps differently. More cover on a policy in force is not checked again.

An application's cover carries its option choices (`ProposedCover.option`, the core's `axis=choice,...` code), which go to the core as the cover's `excess_option`. AIA Living proposals have AIA's own interim cover (`Settings.interim_by_product`: `aia-interim`, cover `life`, up to $500,000) in place of the general interim product. (`tests/eligibility.rs`.)

## A joint first-event cover

A product cover with `joint_first_event` (core, 13 Nov 2026) is one cover with a layer per life. Underwriting needs nothing special for it: each life discloses and is decided on its own, a loading or exclusion stays on that life's part of the cover, and the policy is issued only when every life is settled (a declined life makes the others confirm before issue). Capacity is reserved per life: the design counts a joint cover in full against each life's retention (agreed with reinsurance, 14 Nov); only a treaty declaring a joint basis would need more. No shipped product declares a joint cover yet. (Driver test `a_joint_cover_carries_each_lifes_own_terms`.)

## A future insurability option

Built 14 November 2026 against the core's `ExerciseOption` (core 702a286). The portal or policy servicing opens `open_proposal` as `new-business` with entry `{entry: "insurability_option", policy, event, event_date, evidence}`: one life, one cover, the amount wanted, on the policy the option belongs to; `evidence` is where the proof of the event is kept. It is refused `option` otherwise, or when the event is dated after the day of the request. No health questions are asked: the life's disclosure is refused, and a case opens on the financial queue (reason RC-0977) for a person to check the proof. The person holding the case sends `check_option_evidence` (`case`, `underwriter`, `accepted`, `note`; a refusal needs a note). Accepted, the cover is decided with no underwriting (source `insurability_option`, the event and who checked it), capacity is reserved as for any increase, there is no offer, and the driver sends `ExerciseOption` to the core (key `{proposal}/option/{life}/{cover}`, reference the issue key). The core checks the window, age, uses, share and money limits and writes the layer on the terms of the life's original cover; its refusals (`option_window`, `option_age`, `option_used`) leave the proposal issue failed for a person. Not accepted, the request ends withdrawn and the policyholder is told (RC-0978). (`src/option.rs`, `tests/option.rs`, the driver test `an_accepted_option_is_exercised_on_the_policy_in_force`.)

## A broker underwriting a scheme's members

Agreed with group administration on 13 November 2026, after Mitchell asked for broker underwriting authority up to a limit. A scheme's terms name a delegated authority, and each member's `group_excess` open on that scheme carries `delegated_authority`. The open is refused `authority` when no authority of that ID is in force. When the authority covers the product and every cover is within its per-life ceiling for the cover type, and the proposal is not a takeover already decided as it opened, the module asks the delegate with `request_delegated_decision` (proposal, life, party, cover, cover type, amount, authority) on the `underwriting-delegates` hub, kind `delegated_decision_request`, and marks the proposal as the delegate's: the member's disclosure to us is refused `delegated`. The delegate answers with `delegated_decision` from `delegate-api`, which binds inside the authority as any delegated decision does. A decision outside it is rejected with its reason, the life goes to our queue and the proposal is no longer the delegate's. Above the ceiling, or on a product the authority does not cover, the excess runs as any other. Group never names an authority for a member reinsurance capped or on a facultative scheme. (`special.rs` `ask_delegate`, `tests/delegated.rs`.)

## Known gaps

- **Letters go to documents; evidence orders and delegate file requests go to their own hubs.** A letter to a party or an adviser is a `request_letter` to module `documents` (caller `underwriting`, request and key `uw:{outbox number}`, the kind, the recipient, the proposal as subject, the life, the reason codes and the business date; agreed with the documents thread on 13 November 2026). Documents renders template `uw-{kind}`, treats offers and declines as clinical class and refuses them to an adviser (`clinical_to_adviser`); a refusal parks the letter. The letter carries no policy ID yet. An adviser recipient must be the adviser's party ID in the customer master. Evidence orders and delegate file requests go to the `underwriting-evidence` and `underwriting-delegates` hubs, whose readers are the providers' and delegates' adapters (not built here). Staff notices go to the event hub, whose resources are in `infra/underwriting.bicep` (compiled in CI, not yet deployed); until it is deployed each staff notice is parked with the hub's refusal (a missing hub or a refused identity parks; a busy or unreachable hub is tried again on the next pass).
- **The clinical store and the intake have not been deployed.** `infra/underwriting.bicep` (key, erasable container, clinical identity, its right to read the cluster token) and `deploy/k8s/underwriting-intake.yaml` are compiled and rendered by CI only. The portal does not yet call the intake: it needs a token for the intake's audience with the app role `new-business`, and a page that has the life sign the hash on their device. Reading a record back is `POST /v1/disclosures/open` (`{request, proposal, life, underwriter, purpose}`, app role `underwriting-console`): the module's `OpenDisclosures` allows someone accredited for the product, with no relationship to anyone on it, who holds or has worked a case on the life, or a senior, and records `FileAccessed` before any record is read; each record's hash is checked against the module's. The portal's underwriting console (`/underwriting` in `ic-portal`, `docs/channels.md`) calls it; the portal's identity holds both `new-business` and `underwriting-console`. Underwriters' profiles must name them by their workforce ID, which the console sends as every actor field.
- **Non-disclosure remedies are carried to the core** (`AvoidPolicy`, `VaryForNonDisclosure`, caller `underwriting`, key `{review}/remedy`, the review as the case). A variation carries the terms the cover is treated as having been written on; an avoidance carries whether the premiums paid are refunded or kept, which the two seniors record with the remedy. Which of the two the law allows for a contract needs legal review; the module does not choose. A refund lands in policy suspense and is not paid out automatically. Any refusal (for example `claim_in_payment`) is parked for a person.
- **Evidence-free reinstatement days** (decided by this thread at Mitchell's request, 2026-11-12). Term life: 60 days of its 180-day window. The policy lapses only after 90 days unpaid, so by then the insurer has been unpaid for five months, long enough for a change in health to matter. Health plan: 30 days of its 90-day window, since it lapses sooner (60 days unpaid) and claims follow health changes quickly. Group life: not set. A scheme member's cover comes back through the membership file, and only cover above the free cover limit is underwritten, as excess. Past the evidence-free days a reinstatement needs a reinstatement proposal here; a clean declaration of health goes straight through the rules, anything else goes to an underwriter. The products were changed in place because no version has been in production; once one has, a change like this is a new product version with its own `effective_from`. Collections may open the reinstatement proposal itself (caller `collections`, which may open only a `reinstatement` entry; `tests/collections.rs`), building it with `reinstatement::reinstatement_proposal` from the lapsed policy read from the core (id `reinstate:{policy}:{lapsed_on}`, the cover held the day before the lapse) when the core refuses `Reinstate` with `underwriting_required`; until the collections module does so, whoever handles the request opens it.
- **A reinstatement reserves no reinsurance capacity**, on the footing that cover inside its reinstatement window is still ceded.
- **Health cover priced per life is underwritten on an amount.** The rules and profiles cover `term-life`, `health-plan`, `health-flex`, `income-protection` and `group-life`. A proposal's cover needs an amount above zero, which for health is what authority limits and reinsurance capacity are measured against; for a cover the product prices per life, whoever opens the proposal (no channel does yet) is to send the cover's largest annual benefit limit, and the core is sent the same figure, which it records and does not rate on. Whether the core should hold zero for such covers is a question for the core thread.
- **An alteration changes underwriting terms only**: loadings and exclusions. A change of occupation also sends the life's new occupation class (`AlteredTerms.occupation_class`, from the proposal's life factors); the core rates the life on it from the business date and keeps the old class for earlier dates. It reserves no reinsurance capacity, since the cover is already ceded and its sum insured does not change; reinsurance follows the ended and new layers from the core's events. A change to a rating attribute held on the life (smoker status, occupation class) or to the sum insured is not part of it. A loading added on an alteration is not referred to the reinsurer again, which a facultative placement may require.
- **A group excess proposal is marked issued by group administration**, which sends `policy_issued` (caller `issuer`, key `{request}/excess-issued`) with the layer the core wrote, so the reservation is confirmed against it. `crates/group/tests/underwriting.rs` drives a member's excess through this module's real state machine and includes `tests/common/mod.rs` by path: renaming `World` or its helpers (`new`, `send_keyed`, `disclose`, `automatic`, `days`, `ok`, `clean`, `now`, `today`, `s.outbox`) breaks that test.
- **Terms refused for `billing_behind`** (the core's day has moved on and its end of day has not run) are sent again by the next daily sweep under a new key, with no notice to anyone. After five attempts in all, or once the proposal's terms are out of date, the proposal is left as `issue_failed` with code `billing_behind_retries_exhausted` and the underwriting manager is told.
- **Interim cover is shipped but off.** `products/interim_accident.star` (`interim-accident`, cover `accidental-death`, no premium, up to $1m) is activated with the other shipped products. Its amount and the accidental death wording are placeholders for product and legal to approve. It is switched on by activating settings with `interim = {product_code: "interim-accident", cover_code: "accidental-death", max_sum_insured_minor: ...}`, which needs an author and two approvers like any setting.
- **Idempotency keys are forgotten after 400 business days** (`KEYS_KEPT_DAYS`), at most a hundred per command; the last 20,000 results are kept to answer repeats in full. A sender that repeated a key after 400 days would have it taken as new. Every sender's keys are named for the day or for an acknowledged effect, so none does. Keys recorded before this was built have no day and are never forgotten.
- **Staff applications.** An authority profile may name the underwriter's own party (`party` in `profile(...)`); nobody acts on an application on which they are the policyholder or a life, declared or not, and the case goes to someone else as for a declared relationship. Whether a staff member's application needs any further approval (for example a senior's signature on any terms) is not set by the design notes and is not checked here. Profiles that do not name a party rely on declarations alone.
- **No Temporal workflow per proposal.** The design describes one; the state machine, the driver and the daily sweep do the same work with one fewer moving part. Timers are therefore accurate to the day, not the hour.
- **Progress rows are published but no page shows them yet.** The channel module holds them in its read models (kinds `proposal` and `proposal_life`); the portal pages for applicants and advisers do not render them.
