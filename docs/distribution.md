# Distribution: adviser commission and held balances

`crates/distribution` (`ic-distribution`) is the module that knows who sold and who services each policy, what commission each layer earns and for whom, what has been paid and what could still be clawed back, and what the insurer holds for or is owed by any counterparty. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md`, and it is hosted on the deployed node as the module `distribution`. Its staff policy is `config/cedar/distribution.cedar`.

## What it does

- **The distribution structure** (`structure.rs`). Parties (advisers, licensed providers, dealer groups, brokers, and counterparties outside distribution such as a reinsurer, an employer, an estate or a liquidator's office), dated relations between them and dated engagements. Everything is effective-dated, so a question asked as at a past date gets the answer that was true then.
- **Licence status as a cascading dated attribute.** A licence record (`active`, `suspended`, `revoked`, `lapsed`) is a dated entry on a party, with the regulator's notice as its source. An adviser's effective status on a date is their own record or, where that is active, the first licence problem found on the chain of licences their engagement depends on that day, so a provider losing its licence reaches every adviser who relies on it without a command per adviser. A licence event opens a conduct case, raises tasks for the policies that need a new servicing adviser, and is the fact every later decision reads.
- **A suspended payee accrues to held.** Commission for a payee who may not be paid (licence, dispute, missing or unverified payment details, legal hold) is still earned and still posted; it goes to the payee's held account with the reason, not to commission payable. Nothing is lost and nothing is paid. A backdated licence event moves what was accrued to payable after its effective date across to held, by entries dated today that cite the event.
- **Commission rules and schedules** (`rules.rs`, `model.rs`, `config.rs`). Rules are data: commission type, component, rate in parts per million, a list of payees with fixed shares and one remainder share, and an optional tail. Schedules give initial and renewal terms and the clawback steps. Every rule, schedule, book and book-level adjustment changes through a change set that one person proposes and that a commission approver and compliance each approve; the proposer cannot approve. A change set can be dry-run against the in-force book before it is approved. A commission option keyed wrongly when a layer was written is corrected by the change `CorrectTerms` (layer, option): every month's commission on the layer is worked out again at the right option's rates under the rule each book had on that day, the differences post today as `commission_corrected` entries (commission expense changes), and the clawback exposure on initial commission scales with it. Where the two options differ in a way earlier commission cannot be scaled to (a renewal rate that was zero, a different renewal start, or initial commission already paid at a rate of zero), the change is refused with `correction_needs_adjustment` and the difference is made with an `Adjustment`.
- **Earning** (`commission.rs`). The module reads the core's event stream. A receipt or an application from suspense carries the net premium by cover type; the module works out the commission fragments for each layer from the terms that layer was written under, rounds once per line with the remainder going to the remainder share, and posts one balanced entry per event. Initial commission creates a clawback exposure that runs off by the schedule. Lapse, cancellation and unwind claw back what the schedule says, to the payee who received it, and a replacement carries the old layer's exposure forward where the layers are linked. Premium waived during a claim earns renewal commission the same way, from the claims event that reports it, unless the schedule the layer was written under sets `earns_on_waived_premium` to false. A policy avoided for non-disclosure (`PolicyStatus::Avoided`) is treated as never in force: every line of commission still standing on it, initial and renewal, is reversed from whoever received it and any clawback given back, whether the premium is refunded or kept. A layer that ends while the policy carries on is clawed back alone, as at the day it ended, or reversed in full inside cooling-off. A layer the core ends and starts again because underwriting changed its terms (`LayerAdded` with `continues`) is the same cover: its terms, books, clock and exposure carry on and nobody is clawed back. Clawback by cause is a switch on a rule, off unless an agreement turns it on: where the rule the commission was paid under has it on and servicing has said which engagement submitted the alteration (`AttributeAlteration`, sent on the day and before the core is asked to carry the alteration out), the clawback the alteration sets off is borne by that engagement's payees, not by the party liable when the commission was paid.
- **Replacements nobody declared** (`replacement.rs`). A replacement is meant to arrive as one (a layer written with `replacement` set, or `LinkReplacement`). Where new cover is simply written on a life after cover on that life was given up, a rule matches the two when the layer is written: same person (the party the core names behind the life), same cover type, inside a window (`replacement_window_months`, 12 by default; zero turns the rule off), and written by the same adviser, by an adviser under the same or a related provider or group, or by anyone where the new amount is the amount given up within a tolerance (`replacement_amount_tolerance_ppm`, 5% by default). The matched amount earns no initial commission and its trail follows the books of the cover it replaced; anything above it is new business. The clawback raised when cover was given up is given back in proportion to the amount matched, and that share of its exposure items carries on under the new layer on their original clock. The writing adviser is told in the answer to `WriteLayer` (`ReplacementMatched`, with the reason) and may dispute it (`DisputeReplacement`, from new business, policy servicing or the adviser portal; the portal must name the adviser in `adviser`, and the module refuses anyone who is not the adviser of an engagement the layer was written under): from then the initial commission on the matched amount is earned into held under the dispute. A person with `case.manage` decides (`DecideReplacement`): it was a replacement and the held commission is taken back, or it was not, in which case the held money is left to be released through the workbench, the layer is new business from then on and the clawback on the cover given up is raised again. The rule also looks back the other way, because a replacement usually happens in that order: an adviser moving a customer who is still in force writes the new cover first and the old cover is cancelled afterwards. When cover is given up, layers already written on the same person in the window before are matched in the same way, the initial commission already paid on the matched amount is taken back from whoever received it, and from then on the matched amount is treated as a replacement. Cover given up inside cooling-off, or ended by a claim, is never matched. Neither is an avoided policy, which was never in force.
- **Books and movement.** Policies sit in books. A layer is written into books by `WriteLayer`; a book that belongs to an engagement takes only layers that engagement helped write (`book_not_writers` otherwise), while a team book or the house book takes anyone's. An adviser with two engagements therefore earns each policy in the book of the one it was written under, and an increase or added cover written by someone else lands in their own book, never in the original adviser's or under a tail. A policy bought direct is written to no book and earns nothing. Scheme cover is formula-driven per member and the core holds no layers for it, so the module keeps one layer per scheme cover, numbered 0 and on no particular life (`{policy}/{cover}/0`), created when the scheme is issued or a member first joins with that cover. The scheme's broker writes it like any layer (group administration may send `WriteLayer`), with the scheme's expected annual premium, and premium the scheme pays for that cover type earns on it. A broker change moves that layer to the new broker's book from the appointment date (`MoveLayers`), and an agreed handover share for the outgoing broker is a dated line in the new book's rule. A broking firm may engage advisers like a provider. Appointing a new servicing adviser, moving a book, merging two books and ending an engagement are planned and applied as dated changes; renewal follows the servicing adviser from the date, and initial commission and its clawback stay with whoever earned it.
- **Statements, sweeps and payment** (`statement.rs`). A statement takes a payee's payable balance up to the period end, applies payment instructions in priority order (court orders and statutory deductions first, entered only by legal), recovers advances, and produces one sweep per partition to clearing and one net payment. A returned payment goes back to held with the reason. A payee with a hold, an unverified account or a licence problem is not paid.
- **Withholding tax on commission** (`statement.rs`, a placeholder until the tax review the design names). Commission paid is treated as a schedular payment: when a statement is paid, or held commission is released through the workbench, tax is withheld at the payee's rate (rounded down) and posted from the payee's clearing account to withholding tax payable (`InternalLedger::WithholdingTax { kind: "schedular" }`, the core's account 2220, posting type `withholding_schedular`), and the payment is the rest. The statement shows `withheld_minor`. A payment the bank returns gives back the tax in proportion, with the money, so the next payment withholds once on the whole. The rate comes from `SetWithholding` (payee administration, or the customer master): a declared rate, or `exempt`. A payee with nothing declared is withheld from at `no_declaration_withholding_bp` (45%) if a natural person (party kind `adviser`), and not at all if a business. The setting `commission_withholding` (on) turns it off. Best estimates to confirm: that the rules cover the payees at all, that businesses are outside them, and the no-declaration rate. The remittance to Inland Revenue is built by payments and the core (`RemitWithholding`, posting type `withholding_remittance_schedular`; `docs/decisions.md`, "Withholding tax"); the schedular payment return is not built (payments and finance).
- **New business attributed to its adviser** (`newbusiness.rs`). New business sends `AdviseNewBusiness { policy, advice: { adviser, provider, option, replacement, premiums } }` (caller `new-business` or `migration`, or staff with `policy.assign`), before or after the core issues the policy. The module writes each unwritten layer itself: the engagement in force between that adviser and that provider on the layer's start, the book that engagement owns, the schedule set for the product (`SetProductSchedule`, by product or by product and cover type, which wins) and the cover's premium. Malformed advice is refused (`invalid_new_business`), a second for the same policy is refused (`already_advised`), and a layer that cannot be resolved (`no_engagement`, `no_book`, `no_schedule`, `no_premium`, or any `WriteLayer` refusal) is listed on a `new_business_unattributed` task while the others are written. `WriteLayer` stays for callers that know the engagement and book, such as group administration and migration.
- **Policies loaded from another system** (`migration.rs`). The core issues a migrated policy and says so at once (`PolicyMigrated`). It is not a new sale: every layer of it, however written, earns trail only, so no initial commission is paid and no clawback clock starts at the cutover, and the replacement rule does not look at it. The old system knows a policy by one agent code: a change set maps each code to an engagement and the book it is paid into (`MapLegacyAgent`), and the migration load sends `MigrateCommission { policy, agent_code, option, premiums, last_paid }` per policy (caller `migration`). The module writes each unwritten layer from the code under the product's schedule, which also sets the servicing adviser, and records the assumption that the single code is writer, servicer and book for every cover. It then works out a year of renewal commission on the premiums for each payee and compares it with what the old system last paid (`last_paid`, a year per payee): a difference of more than one dollar or one per cent raises a `migration_commission_mismatch` task. The record, with every payee's two figures, is the question `migration { policy }`. Refusals: `not_migrated`, `unknown_agent_code`, `no_premium`, `no_schedule`, `invalid_migration`; a layer already written is skipped, so a load refused part way is sent again once the cause is fixed.
- **Starter schedules for the AIA products** (`config/distribution/aia-schedules.json`, placeholders). One change set the distribution administrator proposes and approves through the console: `aia-risk` (life, trauma, TPD: upfront 200% and 10%, hybrid 120% and 20%, level 30%), `aia-income` (upfront 150% and 10%, hybrid 100% and 15%, level 25%), `aia-simplified` (Essentials Life: upfront 100% and 10%, level 20%), `aia-health` (upfront 100% and 15%, level 20%, a 12-month clawback) and `aia-group` (level 10%, no clawback). Retail schedules claw back all in the first year and half in the second, with a 30-day cooling-off. The same change set names each product's schedule (`SetProductSchedule`; income cover on AIA Living under `aia-income`), and `aia-interim` (free cover) earns none. The rates are best estimates of the New Zealand adviser market, not agreed terms; each is replaced by a new schedule version once the distribution agreements are signed.
- **The held balance workbench** (`workbench.rs`). This is the general counterparty facility, not an adviser feature. For any party it shows the position (payable, held by reason and age, receivables, retention, advances, exposure), a proof of debt as at a date, and the open actions. Every change is a typed action: `release`, `offset`, `hold_pending`, `write_off_held`, `write_off_debt`, `split`, `record_dividend`, `move_to_guarantor` and `set_recovery`. An action is prepared by one person and signed by another; legal signs where the party has ceased or a legal hold applies, and the conduct officer and legal both sign before a recovery that was held, because the insurer made the error and the recipient acted in good faith, is moved to pursued. Amounts are checked against the preparer's and the approver's authority limits, the action is checked again when it is applied (the balance may have moved), and nothing is paid to an account that is not verified.
- **The daily aged control.** `RecordDailyControl` takes a snapshot of every held balance by reason and age. A held balance past the threshold with no documented decision (a `hold_pending` with a review date in the future and a legal reference) is an exception, and exceptions block month-end.
- **Staff authorisation** (`authority.rs`). Roles (`distribution_admin`, `commission_ops`, `commission_approver`, `compliance`, `counterparty_finance`, `counterparty_finance_approver`, `legal`, `conduct_officer`) come from templates and dated grants. Every action answers what, whose, how much and why through the Cedar policy, default deny, and every decision, allowed or refused, is recorded with the reason. A refusal for want of a role names the action, says it in words and says who does it ("none of your roles allows payee.hold (holding or releasing a payee's payments): that is done by the distribution administrator or compliance"), and a declared conflict of duties says so. A payee's payments are held and released (`set_payment_hold`, action `payee.hold`) by the distribution administrator or by compliance, so an adviser under investigation stops being paid without a licence event. A hold on a party that is not a payee names the payee its earnings are paid to, or says to register it first. Question `parties` lists every party in the structure with its kind, its own licence on the day, whether it has ceased, and whether it is a payee and held: the commission console picks parties from it.

## How it talks to the rest

**In.** Core events, one per command, with the event ID as the idempotency key (`host::react`); a duplicate is ignored. Staff commands arrive through the module command route as an envelope of key, actor and command. Other modules use the same commands for their own counterparties: claims for an overpayment, reinsurance for a recovery due, group administration for scheme arrears each post a receivable and describe it with `DescribePosition`.

**Out.** Money moves only by the core command `PostCounterparty`, added for this module and for the finance side of the workbench. Each posting has a stable reference that is its idempotency key in the core, and the module's balances are built only from the `CounterpartyPosted` events that come back, never from what it asked for. A posting the core refuses, or cannot route, is final: it is recorded against the thing that asked for it, raises a task for a person, and is not offered again.

**Questions** (`host::Question`): a party's position, proof of debt, a statement, the held control, month-end blocks, clawback exposure, a change set's dry run, a planned move, unconfirmed postings, an action, a case, open tasks and status. For a publisher there are `changed_parties` (the parties that moved after a module sequence, with the sequence to ask from next) and `engagements` (all or one adviser's, each with the licence problem that stops it being used on the day asked) `relations` (every dated link in the structure) and `conduct_cases` (each case a licence event opened, with the event and the advisers, payees and policies it reaches). For oversight and conduct reporting, `replacement_matches` lists every layer the replacement rule matched, with who wrote it, the reason, and whether it is disputed or was overturned. `conduct_facts {from, to}` gives finance's conduct measures their facts, dated the business day each happened and oldest first: `replacement_written` for each layer declared a replacement when written or matched by the rule, and `replacement_overturned` (with `relates_to`, the date of the match) for each match a dispute decided for the adviser. Each is recorded on the layer when it happens, never changed, and carries an ID unique for ever (`{layer}/replacement:{n}`, `{layer}/overturned:{n}`), the product code and no personal information, so the close driver can send the same facts again and finance counts each once. Two are shaped for the finance module's month-end: `aged_held` returns the aged items list in the form `ReportAgedItems` takes for kind `held_balance`, and `sweep_check` answers the `commission_sweep` close check.

## Known gaps

- **Delivery has not been run against a live cluster.** On a deployed node the module's group receives every partition's events through the host, and the host carries the module's outbox to the core (`host::OUTBOX`): oldest first, each posting keyed by its own reference, removed only when its outcome is heard or the core's own event confirms it. The tests drive this through the same functions the host calls, in the simulated cluster; it has not yet run on the multi-node cluster in the Docker cycle.
- **Other modules pull; this one does not push.** The host carries a module's commands to the core, not to another module, so each neighbour reads this module's questions from its own process: the portal's forwarder (`crates/channels/src/forward.rs`) publishes the workbench, the adviser directory and licence status and replays approved workbench actions one signature at a time; the finance driver (`crates/finance/src/driver.rs`, `distribution_sources()`) reports `sweep_check` and `aged_held` to month-end and passes `conduct_facts` on as `RecordConductFacts` from caller `distribution` (return lines C9 and C10); the complaints relay (`crates/documents/src/complaints_relay.rs`) opens the conduct case from `conduct_cases`. None of the three depends on this crate, so a change to the shape of an answer must be agreed with its reader first.
- **Commission is posted by its own entry** after the premium event, not as lines in the premium entry. The entry cites the event and is idempotent on it, but the two are not atomic.
- **Renewal commission follows refunded premium only where billing is reversed.** When an unwind or a reopened choice reverses the billing of periods already paid, the core says how much premium went back by cover type (`PremiumUnapplied`) and the renewal commission it earned is taken back from the payees who received it. A refund of money that was only ever in suspense earned nothing and takes nothing. A credit adjustment (a reduction in cover, a scheme correction) that leaves paid premium in suspense is reported by the core the same way and takes its renewal commission back; one that only reduces what is still owed needs nothing, because commission is earned on cash received.
- **Only claims' receivables are shown from another module's ledger.** A claim overpayment stays in the claim ledger; the workbench shows it on the party's position as a memo line from claims' own events (claim, amount outstanding, recovery status, whether the insurer made the error), outside this module's balances and control totals. Decisions on it are sent straight to claims with the role `held-balances`. A release that would leave less held than claims is actively collecting from the party (a recovery that is pursued or being offset) needs legal's signature, and one prepared before the debt appeared is refused at its last signature with `owes_elsewhere` until it is prepared again. The business can instead refuse such a release outright by approving the change `ReleaseOwedElsewhere(Refused)`; `NeedsLegal` is the default. The module cannot offset against the claim ledger itself; that is done in claims. Reinsurance recoveries and scheme arrears are not shown unless their owners post them with `PostCounterparty`.
- **Volume-based overrides are not built, on purpose.** The design notes leave them out: one would be a book-level item and would need checking against the conduct rules on incentives first.
- **Limits of the replacement rule.** Where the cover was reduced and not removed, the clawback on the part cut stands even if it is matched later. Trail already paid to the original book before a match is overturned is not moved. A policy reinstated after its cover was matched raises a task for a person and is not reversed automatically. The 12-month window and the 5% tolerance were confirmed by Mitchell on 13 November 2026; both are settings and can be changed.
- **Clawback by cause depends on servicing saying who altered the policy.** The core's alteration commands carry no engagement, so the channel that submits an alteration sends `AttributeAlteration` first. Since 15 Nov the portal does, for a cancellation an adviser submitted (`docs/channels.md`, "Carrying servicing requests to the core"). The portal carries no reduction of cover yet, so nothing attributes one.
- **Rules and schedules are data, not Starlark.** They are versioned and go through the change set. Each rule carries the agreement's worked examples (`Rule.examples`: a date, a commission type, a gross amount and premium, and every payee and component paid with the amount). A change that activates a rule is refused (`rule_examples`) unless it has at least one example, every example is dated on or after the rule takes effect, and the rule reproduces every example exactly, nothing more and nothing less; the refusal says what the rule pays and what the example says. The check runs at proposal and again at activation. A book that moves with an adviser brings examples for its new rule in `BookOutcome::Moves.examples`, tail included. The setting `rule_examples_required` (on by default) can be turned off for a migration that loads rules from an older system; examples given are checked either way. Schedules have no examples of their own: a schedule's rate is checked through the rules' examples.
- **One currency.** Amounts are New Zealand dollars in minor units.
- **History is bounded** (`history.rs`). Once in each business month, detail older than `history_days` (seven years by default, matching the retention Mitchell confirmed for most records; zero keeps everything) that nothing can reach any more is let go of. A policy that ended before the cutoff leaves whole (its layers, exposures, allocations, receipts and premium records) when nothing is held from it, nothing is on its way to the core, and no other policy's replacement match or link points at it. Movements already on a statement fold into a brought-forward balance per party and account, which the proof of debt shows as its first line. Statements go except each payee's latest; confirmed postings go except adjustment references, which are kept so a reference cannot be reused; and fully released held items, finished actions, closed cases, done tasks, daily controls, settled memo lines, document records and command keys go. Balances never change. The question `history` says where line detail starts, what is brought forward and what the last run let go of. The detail itself stays in the core's ledger, and issued statements with the documents module. A proof of debt for a date before the cutoff is answered from the core's ledger, not from here.
- **No screens.** Staff commands go through the generic module command route; the portal thread owns the pages.
