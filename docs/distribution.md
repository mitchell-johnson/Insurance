# Distribution: adviser commission and held balances

`crates/distribution` (`ic-distribution`) is the module that knows who sold and who services each policy, what commission each layer earns and for whom, what has been paid and what could still be clawed back, and what the insurer holds for or is owed by any counterparty. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md`, and it is hosted on the deployed node as the module `distribution`. Its staff policy is `config/cedar/distribution.cedar`.

## What it does

- **The distribution structure** (`structure.rs`). Parties (advisers, licensed providers, dealer groups, brokers, and counterparties outside distribution such as a reinsurer, an employer, an estate or a liquidator's office), dated relations between them and dated engagements. Everything is effective-dated, so a question asked as at a past date gets the answer that was true then.
- **Licence status as a cascading dated attribute.** A licence record (`active`, `suspended`, `revoked`, `lapsed`) is a dated entry on a party, with the regulator's notice as its source. An adviser's effective status on a date is their own record or, where that is active, the first licence problem found on the chain of licences their engagement depends on that day, so a provider losing its licence reaches every adviser who relies on it without a command per adviser. A licence event opens a conduct case, raises tasks for the policies that need a new servicing adviser, and is the fact every later decision reads.
- **A suspended payee accrues to held.** Commission for a payee who may not be paid (licence, dispute, missing or unverified payment details, legal hold) is still earned and still posted; it goes to the payee's held account with the reason, not to commission payable. Nothing is lost and nothing is paid. A backdated licence event moves what was accrued to payable after its effective date across to held, by entries dated today that cite the event.
- **Commission rules and schedules** (`rules.rs`, `model.rs`, `config.rs`). Rules are data: commission type, component, rate in parts per million, a list of payees with fixed shares and one remainder share, and an optional tail. Schedules give initial and renewal terms and the clawback steps. Every rule, schedule, book and book-level adjustment changes through a change set that one person proposes and that a commission approver and compliance each approve; the proposer cannot approve. A change set can be dry-run against the in-force book before it is approved.
- **Earning** (`commission.rs`). The module reads the core's event stream. A receipt or an application from suspense carries the net premium by cover type; the module works out the commission fragments for each layer from the terms that layer was written under, rounds once per line with the remainder going to the remainder share, and posts one balanced entry per event. Initial commission creates a clawback exposure that runs off by the schedule. Lapse, cancellation and unwind claw back what the schedule says, to the payee who received it, and a replacement carries the old layer's exposure forward where the layers are linked. Premium waived during a claim earns renewal commission the same way, from the claims event that reports it, unless the schedule the layer was written under sets `earns_on_waived_premium` to false. A layer that ends while the policy carries on is clawed back alone, as at the day it ended, or reversed in full inside cooling-off.
- **Books and movement.** Policies sit in books. Appointing a new servicing adviser, moving a book, merging two books and ending an engagement are planned and applied as dated changes; renewal follows the servicing adviser from the date, and initial commission and its clawback stay with whoever earned it.
- **Statements, sweeps and payment** (`statement.rs`). A statement takes a payee's payable balance up to the period end, applies payment instructions in priority order (court orders and statutory deductions first, entered only by legal), recovers advances, and produces one sweep per partition to clearing and one net payment. A returned payment goes back to held with the reason. A payee with a hold, an unverified account or a licence problem is not paid.
- **The held balance workbench** (`workbench.rs`). This is the general counterparty facility, not an adviser feature. For any party it shows the position (payable, held by reason and age, receivables, retention, advances, exposure), a proof of debt as at a date, and the open actions. Every change is a typed action: `release`, `offset`, `hold_pending`, `write_off_held`, `write_off_debt`, `split`, `record_dividend`, `move_to_guarantor` and `set_recovery`. An action is prepared by one person and signed by another; legal signs where the party has ceased or a legal hold applies, and the conduct officer and legal both sign before a recovery that was held, because the insurer made the error and the recipient acted in good faith, is moved to pursued. Amounts are checked against the preparer's and the approver's authority limits, the action is checked again when it is applied (the balance may have moved), and nothing is paid to an account that is not verified.
- **The daily aged control.** `RecordDailyControl` takes a snapshot of every held balance by reason and age. A held balance past the threshold with no documented decision (a `hold_pending` with a review date in the future and a legal reference) is an exception, and exceptions block month-end.
- **Staff authorisation** (`authority.rs`). Roles (`distribution_admin`, `commission_ops`, `commission_approver`, `compliance`, `counterparty_finance`, `counterparty_finance_approver`, `legal`, `conduct_officer`) come from templates and dated grants. Every action answers what, whose, how much and why through the Cedar policy, default deny, and every decision, allowed or refused, is recorded with the reason.

## How it talks to the rest

**In.** Core events, one per command, with the event ID as the idempotency key (`host::react`); a duplicate is ignored. Staff commands arrive through the module command route as an envelope of key, actor and command. Other modules use the same commands for their own counterparties: claims for an overpayment, reinsurance for a recovery due, group administration for scheme arrears each post a receivable and describe it with `DescribePosition`.

**Out.** Money moves only by the core command `PostCounterparty`, added for this module and for the finance side of the workbench. Each posting has a stable reference that is its idempotency key in the core, and the module's balances are built only from the `CounterpartyPosted` events that come back, never from what it asked for. A posting the core refuses, or cannot route, is final: it is recorded against the thing that asked for it, raises a task for a person, and is not offered again.

**Questions** (`host::Question`): a party's position, proof of debt, a statement, the held control, month-end blocks, clawback exposure, a change set's dry run, a planned move, unconfirmed postings, an action, a case, open tasks and status. For a publisher there are `changed_parties` (the parties that moved after a module sequence, with the sequence to ask from next) and `engagements` (all or one adviser's, each with the licence problem that stops it being used on the day asked) `relations` (every dated link in the structure) and `conduct_cases` (each case a licence event opened, with the event and the advisers, payees and policies it reaches). Two are shaped for the finance module's month-end: `aged_held` returns the aged items list in the form `ReportAgedItems` takes for kind `held_balance`, and `sweep_check` answers the `commission_sweep` close check.

## Known gaps

- **Delivery has not been run against a live cluster.** On a deployed node the module's group receives every partition's events through the host, and the host carries the module's outbox to the core (`host::OUTBOX`): oldest first, each posting keyed by its own reference, removed only when its outcome is heard or the core's own event confirms it. The tests drive this through the same functions the host calls, in the simulated cluster; it has not yet run on the multi-node cluster in the Docker cycle.
- **Other modules pull; this one does not push.** The host carries a module's commands to the core, not to another module, so each neighbour reads this module's questions from its own process: the portal's forwarder (`crates/channels/src/forward.rs`) publishes the workbench, the adviser directory and licence status and replays approved workbench actions one signature at a time; the finance driver (`crates/finance/src/driver.rs`, `distribution_sources()`) reports `sweep_check` and `aged_held` to month-end; the complaints relay (`crates/documents/src/complaints_relay.rs`) opens the conduct case from `conduct_cases`. None of the three depends on this crate, so a change to the shape of an answer must be agreed with its reader first.
- **Commission is posted by its own entry** after the premium event, not as lines in the premium entry. The entry cites the event and is idempotent on it, but the two are not atomic.
- **Renewal commission follows refunded premium only where billing is reversed.** When an unwind or a reopened choice reverses the billing of periods already paid, the core says how much premium went back by cover type (`PremiumUnapplied`) and the renewal commission it earned is taken back from the payees who received it. A refund of money that was only ever in suspense earned nothing and takes nothing. A credit adjustment (a reduction in cover, a scheme correction) that leaves paid premium in suspense is reported by the core the same way and takes its renewal commission back; one that only reduces what is still owed needs nothing, because commission is earned on cash received.
- **Only claims' receivables are shown from another module's ledger.** A claim overpayment stays in the claim ledger; the workbench shows it on the party's position as a memo line from claims' own events (claim, amount outstanding, recovery status, whether the insurer made the error), outside this module's balances and control totals. Decisions on it are sent straight to claims with the role `held-balances`. A release does not yet check memo lines before paying held money to a party who owes the insurer elsewhere; the person approving sees them. Reinsurance recoveries and scheme arrears are not shown unless their owners post them with `PostCounterparty`.
- **Not built:** detection of a replacement nobody linked, clawback that varies by cause, and volume overrides.
- **Rules and schedules are data, not Starlark.** They are versioned and go through the change set, but they do not ship worked scenarios that run at load, as products and treaties do.
- **One currency.** Amounts are New Zealand dollars in minor units.
- **Nothing is pruned.** Allocations, movements and statements are kept for the life of the state, so the snapshot grows with the book.
- **No screens.** Staff commands go through the generic module command route; the portal thread owns the pages.
