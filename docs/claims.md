# Claims

`crates/claims` (`ic-claims`) decides whether an event is covered and manages the relationship with the claimant. It never touches money itself. It is a deterministic state machine that lives on the policy's partition, inside the core's replicated state (`CoreState::claims`), so a decision and its postings commit or fail in one command and the claim records are in the partition's snapshot and state hash.

## How it sits with the core

- Commands arrive as `ic_core::Command::Claims(Box<ic_claims::Command>)`. Each claims command names the modules allowed to send it (`allowed_callers`): `claims` for the work of claims staff, `config` for rules and authority, `payments` for bank outcomes, `reinsurance`, `underwriting`, `customer-master` and `held-balances` for what they tell claims, and `end-of-day` for the standing control.
- The module talks to the core through one port, `ic_claims::Core` (`port.rs`): the business date, the cover check, `execute(Instruction)` and `notify`. `crates/core/src/claims.rs` implements it. Each `Instruction` becomes one balanced journal entry. A claims command issues at most one instruction, validates before it, and changes its own records only after the core accepts, so a refusal leaves nothing behind.
- The core does not take the module's word for where the money is. It keeps its own record per claim on the policy (`Policy::claims`: reserve, payable, released and waiting for the bank, receivable, payments) and refuses an instruction that record does not support. Reconciliation checks every day that the ledger equals the core's record (`claims_ledger`) and that the core's record equals the module's (`claims_control`).
- Facts for other modules are published as `Event::Module { module: "claims", kind, subject: <claim id>, detail }`. They carry IDs, amounts, dates and status. The cause of claim, reason codes and evidence never leave the module, with one deliberate exception: `admission_reversed` carries the reversal's reason code and finding for reinsurance.

## What it does

- **Intake** (`intake.rs`). Every channel lands as `Notify`. A claim is always registered, however doubtful cover looks, with a first reserve posted the same moment. Triage routes by skill: health, lump sum, income protection new and ongoing, group, and a senior queue for large, contested, overseas, multi-policy and non-disclosure cases, which opens a case conference. A provider's health invoice inside the limits is priced and admitted by the rules with no person; any doubt (an exclusion that matches or cannot be read, premium owing, a life not on the policy) sends it to a person. Pre-approval is a hold against the limits with an expiry, not a posting.
- **Service timers.** Acknowledgement, first contact, decision and payment each have a target by line. A missed target escalates to the owner, the queue lead and the claims manager on set days. The decision target doubles for a vulnerable claimant. Timers and reviews fall due by business date at the cutoff and after each end-of-day chunk; nobody has to remember them.
- **Assessment.** One owner at a time. Someone who underwrote the life or has declared a relationship with anyone on the claim is never offered it and cannot act on it. The cover check stores the facts as at the event date and which were relied on. Evidence is requested with a due date and chased. The clinical file opens only to the owner or a reviewer with an open review, and every view is logged with its purpose.
- **Decisions** (`decide.rs`). Reserve, admit, decline, withdraw, reverse admission, ex gratia, end benefit, recovery status and write-off are each a decision type with its own authority. A decision records the signers, the profile, the rule and authority versions, the cause code, what the rules made of each exclusion, any override, and the definitions and facts relied on. Amount limits can be borrowed from a second signer whose own profile covers the whole decision; the power to decline, to override an exclusion, to reverse an admission and to change a recovery status cannot. Checks run again when the last signature is given.
- **Paying** (`book.rs`). A payee is verified first. A lump sum is released by its owner inside their payment authority. Benefits in payment are a schedule: runs are prepared by one person with a count and a control total and approved by a lead who is someone else; a payment the core refuses is an exception with a task and the rest of the run goes on. Claims releases a payment; the payments module settles it at the bank with `SettlePayable`, which is refused unless claims released exactly that payment. A bank return puts the money back in benefit payable and raises a task.
- **Handling costs** (`book.rs`, `PayCost`). The cost recorded on a piece of received evidence or a completed rehabilitation task is paid to its provider once, by the claim's owner within their payment authority, for exactly the recorded amount. The core expenses it to `claims_handling` for the product and owes it in the policy's benefit payable, so the payments module settles it like any released payment; it needs no admission and is never counted as benefit paid. The event is `claim_cost_paid` (policy, payment_ref, net_minor, payee, kind, item), apart from `claim_paid` so nobody reads a fee as a benefit. A returned cost is owed to the provider again and cannot be paid to the claimant; paying it again releases the money that came back and expenses nothing.
- **Interest on late payment** (`Rules.late_interest`). Where the rules set a yearly rate and a number of free days, a lump sum released by hand after the free days carries simple interest on the amount paid, from the end of the free days after the admission to the day of release, by days over 365 and rounded down. The core posts it in the entry that releases the payment (`claim_late_interest`: claim interest expense to benefit payable) and instructs one payment for benefit plus interest, so the bank settles the total. `claim_paid` shows the benefit as `amount_minor`, the total as `net_minor` and the interest as `interest_minor`; interest is never counted as benefit paid. Once a payment has come back from the bank, the claim accrues no more: the returned payment is sent again with the interest it had. Benefit periods, provider runs, ex gratia payments and handling costs carry none; a late benefit period is a matter for arrears or an ex gratia decision. Where the rules keep back resident withholding tax (`Rules.interest_withholding`, off by default because the law decides), each payment withholds it from the interest at the payee's rate (`SetWithholdingRate { claim, rate_bp }`, from the customer master's `resident_withholding_rate`, which the owner records once the payee is verified), or at `Rules.no_declaration_rate_bp` when no rate was given. The amount is rounded down and sent as `Instruction::Pay.interest_withheld_minor`; the core posts it in its own `tax_withheld` entry and tells the bank to pay the rest. `claim_paid` then shows the interest and the net after the tax, `Payment.interest_withheld_minor` records the tax, and the report totals it (`interest_withheld_minor`). A reissued payment is sent for exactly what came back, with nothing withheld a second time. Tax on income benefits themselves is kept back at `Rules.benefit_withholding_bp` of each period's benefit (never more than is left after an offset), carried on the run item and the period (`tax_withheld_minor`), sent as `PayBenefit.tax_withheld_minor` and taken off what the bank pays; a returned period is reissued for what came back, and the report totals it (`benefit_tax_withheld_minor`). The rate is a placeholder of zero, the best estimate until the tax review the design names: New Zealand income benefits are paid gross and taxed in the payee's own return.
- **Premium waiver** (`StartWaiver`, `Instruction::WaivePremium`). Billing is untouched: the premium is billed as usual. While a waiver runs, claims asks the core at once and then at every day end to pay from claims expense (`premium_waived`: claims incurred for the cover, posting type `premium_waiver`, to premium receivable) whatever is owing for periods that start inside the waiver's window, which runs from the date the owner gave to the end of the benefit. Earned premium and claims cost are both recognised, the policy owes nothing and keeps its standing, and arrears from before the window are left alone. The core keeps the total on the claim's ledger record (`waived_minor`) and publishes `premium_waived` (policy, amount_minor). The owner of a benefit in payment starts a waiver beside it; a claim of type premium waiver is admitted with a monthly amount of zero, starts waiving from its first period without being asked, is never in a payment run, and ends itself on the first day outside its window. Where the product says the cover has no waiver (`CoverFacts.premium_waiver` is `false`), `StartWaiver` and the admission of a waiver-only claim are refused with `no_waiver_cover`; where the product says nothing, whether the cover includes waiver is the owner's judgement from the wording. Limits: a waived premium is not a receipt, so no premium receipt is sent; the distribution module earns renewal commission on it from `premium_waived`, which also carries the premium by cover type (`net:<cover_type>`), unless the commission schedule says otherwise; reversing the admission stops the waiver from that day; a reversal for a false declaration also bills back everything the waiver paid (`Instruction::UnwaivePremium` from the waiver's start: the core posts `premium_waiver_reversed` and the receivables are owed again), while a reversal for the insurer's own error leaves it waived; a policy that is not in force has nothing waived.
- **Lenders and assignees** (`PayInterest`). The cover check carries the interests recorded on the policy and in force on the event date (`CoverFacts.interests`: party, extent, priority). When a lump sum is admitted, each interest takes its share of the proceeds after any premium deducted, in priority order and up to its extent; an interest with no extent takes everything left. The shares are fixed on the claim at that moment. The claimant cannot be paid any of that money (`interest_first`), an interest of lower priority waits for a higher one, and each party is paid its whole share in one payment within the owner's payment authority. The payment is an ordinary `claim_paid` to that party with no account reference, so the payments module pays the lender as a payee of its own. A payment the bank returns is owed to that party again. The interests come from the core's assignment record (`policy.interests`, ranked first notice first) as it stands on the event date. Limits: the core does not restrict claim payments itself, so the order is enforced by claims alone; benefits in payment and health claims are not diverted; a payment to an interested party carries no late payment interest.
- **Nominated beneficiaries** (`CoverFacts.nominations`). On a death claim, what is left after recorded interests is shared among the nomination the cover check returns for the date of death, in its whole-percent shares (the last nominee takes the rounding when the shares make 100), as `InterestDue` entries marked `nominated` and paid with `PayInterest` after every lender; anything not nominated goes to the estate or other payee. `interest_to_be_paid` carries `basis` (`interest` or `nomination`). Shares over 100% in all, or a share of 0, refuse the admission as `invalid_nomination`. The core fills `nominations` from its nomination record (`Command::Nominate`, caller policy-servicing, both timelines) as at the date of death.
- **Continuation policies** (`CoverFacts.continuation_of`). A claim on a policy issued under a leaver's continuation option is assessed on that policy and carries the scheme policy and member it continues (`Claim.continued_from`): `claim_reserved` names them (`continued_from_policy`, `continued_from_life`) for reinsurance, and the working view lists the member's claims on the scheme (`scheme_claims`, from this partition). The core fills `continuation_of` from `IssuePolicy.continuation_of`, which group administration sets when it issues the continuation policy.
- **Benefit statement lines** (`ClaimantView.benefit_lines`). Each paid period shows the benefit before other income, the other income declared for it (accident compensation, a part-time wage; `Period.other_income_minor`, as known when paid), arrears paid with it, any overpayment offset and what was paid, each as its own figure.
- **Proof of life** (`Rules.proof_of_life_months`, `proof_of_life_grace_days`). Where the rules set an interval, every benefit in payment has a proof of life check on its review plan. When it falls due a task opens; the owner closes it only with the proof on file (a certificate, a signed declaration or a death register match, by its vault reference). If the grace passes first, the benefit is held: the claim stays in payment, benefit runs leave it out, a senior review opens and `review_overdue` is published for the contact escalation. Nothing is cancelled, so the first run after the proof is filed pays every held period in full. Limits: there is no annuity product yet, so this serves income benefits today and annuities when the core has them; the customer master can meet it without a person, after an identity check that saw the living person (document and selfie, liveness in the app, in person or by video call, made within 30 days): it sends `ConfirmAlive { party, source, evidence_ref }` (caller `customer-master`, to every partition), which completes every open proof of life on that person's benefits in payment with outcome `confirmed:<source>` and the evidence reference, and publishes `proof_of_life_confirmed`. A death register search proves a death, not a life, so it never meets the check by itself.
- **Moving with the policy** (`ClaimsState::moving`, `detach`, `attach`). When a policy moves to another group's partition, its claims travel in the transfer record with their schedules, timers, pre-approvals and health limits used, and the claim reserve, benefit payable and recovery receivable balances move with the policy's other accounts. While the move is in flight the claims are in no index on either side, so nothing can be done to them (`unknown_claim`); on landing they are registered again exactly as they left, released payments settle on the new partition and the next run there pays the next period. A move that is called off puts them back. Only a claim sitting in a prepared run holds a move back (`claim_in_run`), until the run is approved or cancelled. Limits: a run's own record stays on the partition that paid it, so a run confirmed after the move is confirmed there; an overpayment being offset against another policy's benefit is shared from its new partition only once its position next changes; until then the old partition sends back whatever other partitions keep for it.
- **Migration** (`LoadMigrated`, caller `migration`). A claim from the system before this one is loaded at the step it had reached: open, admitted and unpaid, in payment, closed or declined, with its history, schedule, overpayments and (for closed health claims) the limits it used. The core opens its outstanding reserve, unpaid admitted money and overpayment as one entry (`claim_migrated`) against the claims migration control account, whose balance is reconciled to the old system's outstanding claims; nothing is expensed again. The claim is marked `migrated`, its reviews and waiver go on the calendar, and the next run pays the period after the last one the old system paid. A record that is not consistent is refused whole (`invalid_migration`): negative money, more payable than admitted, a claim in a run or with a decision waiting, a status that does not fit its schedule or money. Limits: payments released and not yet settled in the old system are not carried, so load after its last run has settled; the loader takes the claim in this module's own shape, and mapping a legacy extract into that shape is the migration programme's work.
- **Actuarial reserves for claims in payment** (`ApplyValuation`, caller `config`). The first reserve is still `Rules.initial_reserve_pct` of the amount claimed. After that, the reserve for a claim in payment is the appointed actuary's figure for the expected remaining payments, as the design says. A valuation arrives in parts of at most 500 claims, each with a version, a part number, an as-at date, the basis and a preparer and two other approvers. It is sent to every partition. Each partition sets the reserve of the claims in payment it holds through the ordinary reserve entry (reason `valuation:<version>:<basis>`), publishes `claim_reserve_changed` with the version, skips claims held elsewhere and names the claims it holds that are not in payment in `valuation_applied`. A part sent again is a no-op, the same part with other figures or an older version is refused. The valuation itself is calculated outside the platform from the extracts.
- **Overpayments** (`overpay.rs`). An overpayment is found by replaying paid periods under corrected facts (declared income, an earlier end, a death) and is recorded as a receivable with the status `held`. A shortfall found the same way is owed to the claimant and paid with the next benefit in the ordinary run; a senior is told when no further benefit is due or the person also owes an overpayment. Nothing is asked of anyone until a decision changes the status. An offset is taken only from an overpayment whose status is `offset`, at no more than the agreed share of each benefit, across the same person's claims on the partition and, where the rules name the partitions, on the others (below). Each change is published with the whole position (claim, policy, overpayment, party, outstanding, status, cause, whether it was the insurer's error, good faith); the distribution module shows it on the party's held balance position as a memo line, and decisions on recovery come straight to claims with the role `held-balances`. The receivable itself stays in the claim's own ledger account.
- **Restricted investigations** (`investigate.rs`). A health invoice that bills the same service code on the same service date from the same provider as another claim on the partition opens a case (rule `duplicate_billing`) over both claims. Across partitions (`Rules.partitions` above one), each health claim that names its service tells every other partition (`BilledElsewhere`, caller `partition`). A partition opens a case on its own claim for the same service, whether its claim or the other's came first, and the case lists the claims elsewhere (`elsewhere` on the case view). Each partition's investigator works its own claim. The services billed, here and elsewhere, are kept for ever. An event no more than `Rules.early_claim_days` after the cover started (`CoverFacts.cover_start`; zero turns the rule off) opens a case over that claim (rule `early_claim`). The compliance module refers other patterns, such as altered documents, with `ReferForInvestigation { case, rule, claims }` (caller `compliance`; a repeat is a no-op). While a case is open the rules do not admit its claims and no payment leaves for them (runs leave them out; a manual release is refused as `payment_held` without saying why); to front line staff and the claimant they look like claims waiting for an assessor, with no task, note or view field. Only staff with the `investigator` role see cases (`Question::Cases`, console `GET /staff/v1/claims/cases`, asked as the signed-in person) or close them (`CloseInvestigation`): cleared, or confirmed, which declines the claims named and turns what was paid to a provider on the claims named into a `ProviderBilling` overpayment at 100% offset. A claim can be in more than one case and stays held until the last one closes. `investigation_opened` (case, rule, claim IDs) and `investigation_closed` (outcome) are published with the case as subject.
- **Proceeds held under an insolvency interest** (`Claim.insolvency_hold`, `DirectProceeds`; decision 4 of 13 November, JNT-029). Where the Official Assignee's interest (`Interest.insolvency`) stands on the policy at admission, it is not paid out like a lender's: lenders ranked in the record are paid as usual, and what would go to the owner or the estate is held in benefit payable (`proceeds_held`). A payment to the payee is refused (`held_for_direction`) and benefit runs leave the claim out until legal records a direction naming who to pay (caller `claims`, role `legal`, with the direction's vault reference; `proceeds_directed`); after that only that party, verified as the payee, is paid. Health claims paid to providers are not held. Limit: the hold is read at admission; an interest recorded later, or one released on discharge, does not change a claim already admitted until legal directs.
- **Health counters by cover** (HLT-004, HLT-009). Each health line is priced under its own category's excess, share and limits, and counts against counters kept per policy, life, cover and category (`health_used` keys `policy/life/cover/category`, with `/policy-year` for the year), so one episode billed as several lines on several covers uses each cover's own limits. Where the product's shell declares a counter covers share (`CoverFacts.shared_counter`), that name replaces the cover in the key and one limit serves them all. Pre-approval holds count only against the cover they were given on, or the shared counter.
- **Custody after sentence** (`Request::SuspendBenefit`, `ResumeBenefit`; decision 5 of 13 November, CLM-040). Where the product's wording suspends an income benefit in custody (`CoverFacts.benefit_while_imprisoned`, `suspend` by default, `pay` refuses with `paid_in_custody`), the owner suspends it from the first whole day in custody after sentence, by a decision with its own reason code (the rules map one only to these two kinds), with the evidence's vault reference and a completed conversation with the claimant or their representative. It is never a decline: the claim stays in payment, runs pay nothing for the suspended days (`Schedule.suspended`), the premium waiver carries on, and the reviews wait. Days already paid become an overpayment held as a fact. On release the benefit resumes from the day after, with no new waiting period; the reviews move on by the time they waited, and any day paid as nothing after the release date is owed as arrears. Remand counts only after conviction: the date given is the owner's to choose from the evidence. `benefit_suspended` and `benefit_resumed` carry only the date.
- **Declining late payment interest** (`OfferLateInterest`, `DeclineLateInterest`, `WithdrawInterestDecline`; decision 2 of 13 November, CLM-037). Interest is always worked out and offered: the owner tells the payee the amount owed today (`late_interest_offered`). Only after that can the payee decline, and only by their own decision filed in the vault (`interest_not_offered`, `decision_required`); nothing in a claim depends on it. A payment on a claim with a decline still works the interest out and posts it, and the core reverses it in its own entry (`claim_late_interest_declined`, reason `declined_by_payee`), so the payee is sent the benefit alone and the report counts what was declined (`Report.interest_declined_minor`). Until the claim closes the payee can change their mind: what they declined is paid with the next payment, with interest on that payment as usual.
- **What the record says beyond the sum insured** (`CoverFacts`). A death benefit repays the policy loan with its interest first (`policy_loan_minor`; `Instruction::Admit.repay_loan_minor`, posted in the admission entry from benefit payable to the loan; `Claim.loan_repaid_minor`, `loan_repaid_minor` on `claim_admitted`), then recorded interests, nominees and the estate; a living benefit leaves the loan alone. A cover on two lives that pays once on the first event (`joint_lives`) counts a claim on either life against one sum insured, and a cover reduced by another (`reduced_by`, such as life cover that trauma was accelerated from) counts what the other paid for the same life; the core ends or reduces the covers in the admission entry from its own record: a lump-sum admission that does not end the policy names the life (`Instruction::Admit.life`), and the core takes what the claim admitted off that life's cover, off each cover the product says it reduces, and off every life on a joint first-event cover, handing back premium for the days ahead; reversing the admission (`Close`, outcome `admission_reversed`) writes it back. The facts keep the sum insured gross of these cuts, so claims' own cap counts what was paid once. A funeral advance and a health invoice name no life. The core emits the usual cover events for the cut, and documents raises a schedule trigger from them (one per policy per day); documents issues the new schedule from that trigger (CLM-009 full). A benefit in payment cannot start before the cover's waiting period has run (`waiting_days`, `invalid_schedule`). A health invoice takes the excess the life chose (`chosen_excess_minor`) where the cover offers one, in place of the rules' schedule. Agreed with the core on 13 November; the core fills these fields as it builds loans, joint lives, linked covers and excess options, and until then they are empty and nothing changes.
- **Funeral advance** (CLM-004; `Request::FuneralAdvance { amount_minor }`, `DecisionKind::FuneralAdvance`). On an open death claim, before it is decided, the owner can advance up to `Rules.funeral_advance_max_minor` in all (zero: none) within their admission authority, with no second signature. The reserve falls by the advance (`SetReserve`, reason `funeral_advance`) and the advance is admitted without releasing the rest (`Admit`, `release_reserve: false`), so the claim's cost is unchanged; `funeral_advance_admitted` is published. It is paid with `Pay` to the verified payee, which payments sends as a single payment, and the claim stays open. The admission that follows counts it: it must be more than was advanced (`below_advance`), the core is told only the rest, authority and the second signature are measured on the whole, and `claim_admitted` gives the whole benefit with `advanced_minor`. An advance on a claim later declined is not recovered by the decline; an overpayment can be raised if the claim was false. A `Pay` closes a claim only when it is admitted.
- **What the chosen option sets** (`CoverFacts`, `options.rs` tests). An income benefit cannot be admitted to run past the option's benefit period (`benefit_months`, counted from the first period) or the day its age limit ends it (`benefit_to`, which the core works out from `benefit_to_age` and the life's date of birth): `beyond_benefit_period`. Where the option limits mental health claims (`mental_health_months`, 24 for AIA's `mental-health-limit`), a benefit for a cause tagged `mental` may run that long less the days already paid on earlier mental health claims on the same cover and life (`mental_health_limit`). Where the cover carries claim indexation (`claim_indexation`), the benefit rises at each anniversary of its first period by `Rules.claim_index_bp`, the price index change set in the rules each year and read on the day, compounding on the last rise (`Schedule.index_steps`, `Due::Indexation`, `benefit_indexed`); a period from the anniversary already paid at the old rate is owed the difference as arrears with the next payment, and views and the book show the current benefit. The product's health limits for a category (`health_limits`) replace the rules' field by field, keeping the rules' member share; a category only the product names is paid with no share. A cap on what the policy pays for one event (`event_cap_minor`, AIA Interim Cover's $500,000) limits each admission to what other claims for the same life and event date left. Built-in benefits (`built_in`, each with its kind, its limit resolved against the sum insured, and whether its feature was chosen) are admitted with `Request::AdmitBuiltIn { code, amount_minor, settles }` (`DecisionKind::AdmitBuiltIn`, admission authority, the two-signature threshold on its own amount): refused for an unknown code (`unknown_benefit`), a feature not chosen (`built_in_not_chosen`) or more than its limit has left across the life's claims, the cover and the event cap (`built_in_limit`). An early payment comes off the cover (the `Admit` names the life) and out of the main benefit; the other kinds are paid on top and name no life. The claim stays open for its main benefit and `built_in_admitted` is published, unless `settles` says the built-in benefit is the whole claim, when it is admitted and closes once paid. The core fills these facts from the product and the chosen option (48a2f2e; tested in `crates/sim/tests/aia.rs`); `aia-interim` declares its $500,000 cap as `event_cap`. `AdmitBuiltIn` refuses a `monthly_addition` (`paid_with_the_benefit`: it rides on the schedule, below) and any `facility` or `premium_waiver` (`not_payable`: a service arranged for the claimant), except Business Mentors.
- **AIA income and disablement benefits** (15 Nov 2026; `aia_income.rs` tests). Each applies only where the cover's `built_in` lists it as chosen; a product that lists no built-ins keeps the rules' defaults.
  - *Recurrent Disablement:* where the product lists its built-ins, the recurrence window is 12 months after the claim closed if it has `recurrent-disablement`, and there is none if it does not (`recurrence_window`). Otherwise `Rules.recurrence_days`.
  - *Concurrent Waiting Period:* with `concurrent-waiting-period`, the waiting period runs from the earliest event of an income claim on another policy for the same person (`life_party`) and the same cause code that is in payment, or ended within the last 12 months.
  - *Qualifying period and its waiver:* a total and permanent disablement lump sum is refused before `waiting_days` from the event (`qualifying_period`). `WaiveWaitingPeriod { claim, evidence_ref }` (the owner, an open TPD claim, a cover with `waiver-of-waiting-period`, evidence kept) lifts it: `not_open`, `no_waiver_benefit`, `evidence_required`. `Claim.waiting_waived` holds the evidence.
  - *Partial disablement:* where the product declares `partial-disablement` and it was not chosen, other income above zero is refused (`no_partial_cover`); the benefit is ended instead.
  - *Permanent Disablement Benefit:* `Request::PermanentDisablement { ends }` (`DecisionKind::PermanentDisablement`, admission authority). Accepting needs a benefit in payment (`not_in_payment`), a cover with `permanent-disablement` (`no_permanent_benefit`), not already accepted (`already_started`) and 24 consecutive months paid from the first period with none returned or suspended (`not_24_months`). From that day each period adds a third of the monthly benefit (`Schedule.permanent_from`, `supplement_on`; `permanent_disablement_accepted`). `ends: true` stops it (`not_running` if not paid; `permanent_disablement_ended`).
  - *Peak Season Upgrade:* with `peak-season` (kind `monthly_addition`), each period starting in a month of `CoverFacts.peak_months` adds the built-in's limit (25% of the cover in AIA's definition) before other income is taken off (`Schedule.peak_addition_minor`, `peak_months`).
  - *Business Mentors:* `AdmitBuiltIn` for `business-mentors` while the income benefit is in payment (`not_in_payment`), once for each business (the policy, whichever life), up to the built-in's limit or `Rules.business_mentors_minor`. Rules that leave it out take $1,000, a best-estimate placeholder for a two-year membership; zero refuses (`not_configured`).
  - *Rehabilitation and Support:* the cover check on an income claim with `rehabilitation-support` opens task `rehabilitation/case-manager` due in 5 days and publishes `rehabilitation_case_manager`. Where the product lists its built-ins without it, a rehabilitation task cannot be completed with spending (`no_rehabilitation_benefit`).
  - *Survival period:* a lump sum is refused before `CoverFacts.survival_days` after the event (`survival_period`).
  - *Cover left in place:* where `CoverFacts.ends_cover` is `Some(false)`, a lump sum's `Admit` names no life (the cover is not reduced) and earlier claims do not use up the sum insured.
  - *Not claims':* AIA's Waiting Period Reduction is an alteration to the income cover when a business cover is cancelled, so it belongs to the core and underwriting.
  - *Filled by the core:* `peak_months`, `survival_days`, `ends_cover` and the TPD `waiting_days` are new on `CoverFacts` with serde defaults; until the core fills them from the product, they are empty and the checks they drive do not apply.
- **First notice from the customer** (`channel.rs`). The customer page records a portal request of kind `notify_claim` for module `claims` with payload `{ cover, life, event_date, claim_type, layer? }` (IDs and choices only; unknown fields refused). `channel::notification(request, party, policy, payload)` turns it into a `Notify` from channel `app` and notifier `policyholder`, with the requester as claimant and claim ID `app-<request id>`, or refuses it (`invalid_request`, `policy_required`, `invalid_claim_request`, `health_by_invoice`, `waiver_follows_claim`); `Notify` itself refuses an event after the partition's date. The portal carries it to the policy's partition as caller `claims` and closes the request with `channel::resolution`: applied with the claim ID as reference (also when the claim is already registered, so a retry opens one claim), or rejected with the module's code and message. The claim is then triaged into the assessors' queues like any other. The portal does this since 9b71a70, as soon as a request is filed or approved and in a sweep every `IC_PORTAL_FORWARD_SECS`, with key `notice:<request>`; a policy no partition holds is closed `policy_not_found`, and a partition out of reach leaves the request pending (test `channels/tests/http.rs::a_customers_claim_notice_reaches_claims_once`).
- **Communication marker** (CLM-042; `Flags.communication_support`, set at notification or by `RecordCommunicationNeed { claim, needed }` from the owner or a lead). The claimant deals with one named owner: `claim_assigned` names the owner (`owner`) and carries `communication_support`, and a change of owner on a marked claim records a handover note and opens a call to the claimant for the new owner (`handover/<owner>/<date>`, due in two days). Every deadline the claimant has to meet moves out by `Rules.communication_extra_days`, once: evidence asked of them (`from` is `claimant` or their party) and the grace on each review. Deadlines on others, such as a doctor's report, stay. Turning the marker off shortens nothing already given.
- **Repayment letters** (`Overpayment.letters`, `Question::RepaymentLetters`, `AskRepayment`, `RecordRepaymentLetter`). Claims has no process of its own, so the documents service pulls these letters, the way it pulls dunning notices from collections. A letter asking the payee to repay falls due when recovery moves to pursued or to an arrangement while money is owed, or when the owner or the recoveries queue asks for a reminder (`AskRepayment`; refused as `not_pursued`, or as `letter_waiting` while one has not gone). Each letter has a stable request ID, `cl-op:<overpayment>:<n>`, and `repayment_letter_due` is published. The documents service asks each partition `Question::RepaymentLetters` (oldest first: request, claim, policy, party, life, overpayment, amount, date asked). For each letter it gets the repayment reference from payments, issues template `cl-overpayment-repayment`, and confirms with `RecordRepaymentLetter { claim, overpayment, request }` as caller `documents`, which is idempotent; an unknown request is refused with `unknown_letter`. A letter still waiting when the money is repaid, written off or no longer pursued drops off the list. Claims does not hold the policy's language.
- **Cause category for finance** (FIN-017). `claim_reserved`, `claim_admitted` and `claim_closed` carry `cause_category`: the top segment of the first tag the rules give the claim's cause code (`cancer` for `cancer.skin`). It is left out while no cause is recorded, so a claim reserved at notification shows none and the admission or closing that follows does.
- **Offsets across partitions** (`offsets.rs`, `Rules.partitions`). Where the rules name more than one partition, every non-provider overpayment that is or was in offset is shared with every other partition each time its outstanding amount, share or receipts change (`OffsetSource`, caller `partition`, with a sequence number so an older position never replaces a newer one). A benefit run on another partition takes the same person's local debts first, then the shared ones, within the same cap, and keeps the amount back into offset in transit (core `PayBenefit` with `Offset.partition`, entry `benefit_paid`). It tells the holder (`OffsetTaken`), which takes what is still owed out of transit against the receivable (`ReceiveOffset`, entry `claim_offset_received`) and sends back anything more (`OffsetReturned`): two partitions acting on the same position can overtake the debt. What comes back is taken out of transit and given back to claims expense (`ReturnOffset`, entry `claim_offset_returned`) and owed to the claimant as arrears with their next benefit; a senior review opens where no benefit is coming, and `offset_return_unplaced` is published where the claim has left the partition. Every message is a no-op on repeat. The core's daily reconciliation raises `claims_offset_in_transit` when the account does not net to zero across partitions with no claims message waiting; an unplaced return, or a return the core refused, leaves money there with no message, so the break stays until a person places it, and there is no command yet to place it. Limits: the repeat keys are never pruned; when a policy moves, its overpayment is shared from the new partition only after its position next changes; the partition count is set in the rules and must match the deployment.
- **Provider recovery.** A provider's billing overpayment in offset status is taken out of that provider's next provider run, in full and before anything is paid out (no consumer cap applies). The core posts it in the payment's entry (`claim_offset`: benefit payable to recovery receivable on the policy holding the debt) and instructs only the rest.
- **Views and reports** (`report.rs`): the status view for advisers and employers, the claimant's own view, queues, the book of claims in payment, the management report, the remittance advice for a run, and the totals the daily control compares with the ledger.

## Design addendum

**Entry 5, reversal of admission.**

- `Request::ReverseAdmission` is its own decision type, reported apart from declines (`Report::admissions_reversed`). It needs the senior queue, a case conference and a conversation with the claimant both held since the admission, a proposer who is senior and holds the power, and always a second senior signature. It is refused while the claim waits in a payment run.
- On reversal the benefit stops from the decision date, never before it. Everything paid becomes one overpayment whose cause is the insurer's error and whose recipient acted in good faith, unless the reversal records a false declaration.
- Recovery status is on every overpayment: `held`, `pursued`, `arrangement`, `offset`, `written_off`, `recovered`, with its history. For the insurer's own error and a good-faith recipient, any status other than `held` needs the conduct officer and legal both to sign. The claimant's view shows nothing owing while the status is `held`.
- The finding on the reversal is read from the admission's own record: the assessor overrode a raised exclusion, the cause was recoded after admission, or the rules had no mapping. It opens a quality review.
- The exclusion-match control (`ExclusionControl`, run by `end-of-day` in chunks of 200 with a cursor) compares every claim in payment with the exclusions on its cover as the policy record has them now and sends a match to senior review once per cause and rules version. `ClaimsState::exclusion_hits(&rules)` is the dry run of a proposed rules version against every admitted claim.
- `admission_reversed` and `overpayment_written_off` are published for reinsurance, which raises disputed items; `DisputedItemRaised` records each on the claim. The reinsurance module's driver sends its notices (large claim, consent required, consent, disputed item, dispute resolved) to the partition of the claim's policy as the role `reinsurance`. `payment_returned` says what came back: `ex_gratia`, `cost` and `interest_minor` beside the amount.

**Entry 1, late-notified death.** `RecordDeath` ends every benefit in payment to that person from the day after the death and records what was paid past it, to the day, as an overpayment with the cause late death notification, for recovery from the estate. A claim that cannot be ended at once goes to a senior. A death recorded in error is withdrawn with `RetractDeath` (same party and date, from claims staff or the customer master): a benefit the death had only shortened gets its end date back at once; a claim the death had closed gets a senior review task, and the senior reopens it with the basis `death_retracted`, after which the next run pays every month missed while it was closed. Any overpayment already recorded for the death stays held until a person writes it off. `death_retracted` is published with `benefit` set to `restored` or `to_review`. A death notified more than the configured days late opens a task for a lead to check the cadence of the death register feed and publishes `death_register_cadence_check`. Admitting a death claim on a single-life policy ends the policy as at the event in the same command (`unwind::terminate_on_claim`), after checking it can.

## Configuration

`Rules` and `AuthorityBook` (`config.rs`) are versioned documents activated through the log by the `config` caller on every partition, each with a preparer and two other approvers. A partition with neither refuses claims work with `not_configured`. Decisions record the versions they were made under. Neither is shipped: the authority book names the insurer's own staff, and the limits in the rules are the insurer's to set.

Exclusions have one source. Claims cannot import the underwriting crate (claims state lives in the core, which underwriting depends on), so `Rules.exclusions` is data produced from the underwriting library by `ExclusionLibrary::for_claims`, and a test in `crates/underwriting/tests/libraries.rs` holds the two matchers to the same outcome for every shipped code, cover type and a spread of cause tags. Whoever prepares a claims rules version takes the exclusions from that function.

## Known gaps

- **Health limits are held twice.** The claims rules carry per-event, per-year and lifetime limits by benefit category; the product definition has its own benefit schedule. They are not yet read from one place.
- **Standing on an old event date is only as good as the receivables still held.** The cover check counts a premium as unpaid on the event date when it is still owing or was settled after that date (`Receivable::settled_on`), and reports what has been paid since. The core prunes receivables that are settled and fully earned 400 days after their period ends, so for an event further back than that a premium paid late reads as paid.
- **Read routes.** The read routes are `GET /v1/claims/{claim}` (claims staff, and the person the claim is about under the audience policy: for a death claim the claimant, for any other claim the life insured, even where a company owns the policy and is paid; the audience policy treats the existence of a claim as a health fact), and for the `claims` role only `/v1/claims/{claim}/work` (the working view: owner, tasks, evidence, the decision waiting for signatures, payee, interested parties, payments, benefit, overpayments and timers, with no cause, reason code or vault reference, which are read by `OpenFile`), `/v1/claims/queues/{queue}`, `/v1/claims/book`, `/v1/claims/report` and `/v1/claims/runs/{run}/remittance`. In a cluster each is asked of every partition. The queue and the working view are asked as the signed-in person: a claim they are conflicted on (a declared relationship with anyone on it, or they underwrote the life) is left out of the queue and its working view is not found. Advisers and employers see the status of claims on policies they serve or administer (Mitchell's decision, 13 November 2026: status, never cause). The question `PolicyStatus { policy }` answers every claim on the policy with only its ID, status, notification date and closing date, no type, cause, reason or amount; the portal checks the relationship before asking and serves it on the adviser and employer routes. Claims rows are not yet published to the channels module either.
- **A non-disclosure remedy has nowhere to land.** Claims waits for underwriting's outcome and records it; the core has no vary or avoid command yet.
- **The payments seam has two loose ends.** `claim_paid` becomes a waiting payment in the payments module, which a maker takes into a run and which settles with the `payment_ref` for exactly `net_minor`; `claim_cost_paid` is taken in the same way, and the provider must be a verified payee in the payments module. A payment the bank refuses or returns before it settled comes back to claims as `PaymentReturned` from the payments module, and is released again under a new reference. `claim_paid` names the claims run it went out in (`run`, blank for a payment released by hand) so the payments module can send `ConfirmRun` when every item has settled. Still loose: payments shows the `account_ref` without comparing it with its own payee's account, and an inbound repayment is not tied to an overpayment, so a person with the `claims` role sends `RecordRepayment`.
