# Group scheme administration

`crates/group` (`ic-group`) runs employer schemes: the scheme's terms, the membership files an employer sends, who the members are, cover above the automatic amount, leavers and their continuation option, pool declarations, renewal and ending, and the employer's view of the invoice. It follows `docs/modules.md`.

## What it does

- **Scheme terms as data** (`terms.rs`). Basis (named members, unnamed members, or pools), eligibility, when a leaver's cover ends, backdating the employer may do alone, the safety share, leave of absence, takeover terms, billing groups, continuation products and period, renewal lead time and what happens with no agreement, how underwriting loadings are billed, and when short payment suspends joiners. Terms are checked whole, versioned, and replaced whole.
- **Files** (`csv.rs`, `mapping.rs`, `diff.rs`, `state.rs`). A file is stored in quarantine, scanned, and only then read. It is read with the scheme's sealed mapping (the employer's own column names, date format, salary unit, category and change codes). A full file is compared with membership as the last file left it; a change file is applied row by row. The result is a preview: joiners, leavers, changes, absences, re-keyed IDs, rows rejected with a code and a field name, and how many members would be above automatic cover. One file per scheme is open at a time, so each is compared with the result of the one before. The same content twice is recognised by its hash.
- **Controls on a file.** A full file that would remove more than the safety share of members is held until staff confirm it. The employer approves every file; a scheme can require that the approver is not the uploader. Changes dated further back than the terms let the employer date them wait for staff, who can let them through or drop only those rows. A scheme can be frozen so that it takes and approves no files. A file not finished within the terms' limit is raised to staff.
- **Who a member is (addendum entry 2).** On a named scheme each joiner is matched with the customer master after approval. One high-confidence match links the member to that person; no candidate creates the person; anything between joins the member unlinked, tells reinsurance of each candidate, and raises an exception for staff to decide. The only other way a member is linked is their own verified registration. Names go to the customer master and nowhere else: the module's state holds a salted fingerprint, and its events carry IDs, counts and field names.
- **Applying** (`work.rs`). Approved changes go to the core as `ApplyMembership` in chunks, one command in flight per scheme so the core sees them in the file's order. A busy core is retried the next morning under a new key. A refused chunk is halved until the refused row is alone; that row is reported on the file and raised to staff, and the rest applies. A refusal about the policy itself (lapsed, transferred) stops the scheme's queue with everything in it until staff resume. Changes dated ahead wait and are sent on their day.
- **Cover above the automatic amount.** When the formula gives more than the automatic acceptance limit, the excess is a case sent to underwriting. An accepted decision is written to the core as a second layer for the accepted amount (`AddExcessLayer`); the employer learns only whether it is in place. When salary moves, open requests are withdrawn and layers ended, newest first, until what is held fits the formula, and any new excess is asked for. Reinsurance can require part of a member's automatic cover to be underwritten (accumulation across the member's policies): the member's automatic cover is capped in the core and the rest becomes a case under reinsurance's reference. Loadings are billed as one scheme total, or itemised on the member's line only with the member's recorded consent.
- **Eligibility on every change.** Where the terms set minimum hours, a change that takes a member below them ends cover from that date as for a leaver (the preview shows `hours` as the reason), with the continuation option. When the member qualifies again the employer's next full file, or a join row, brings them back as a new life from that date; a rejoiner with no stated date starts on the file's date.
- **Leavers.** A leaver's layers end with the membership. A leaver young enough gets a continuation option for up to the automatic cover held; taking it up issues a retail policy in the core to the member, starting the day scheme cover ended, with no evidence of health. An option not taken up expires. A leave of absence past the terms' limit ends cover on the terms' date. Members removed in error are reinstated from the day they were removed, with their layers, as if they had not left.
- **Underwriting delegated to the employer's broker** (terms `delegated_underwriting.authority`, the underwriting module's `DelegatedAuthority` id; decided by Mitchell 14 Nov 2026). Each request for a member's cover above the automatic limit names the authority (`delegated_authority` on the `group_excess` entry). Underwriting asks the delegate for a decision on its delegates hub, checks the decision against the authority, and handles anything outside it, or above the authority's per-life ceiling, with our own underwriters. A member reinsurance asked us to underwrite, and a scheme that cedes its excess facultatively, never go to the delegate. While the delegate decides, underwriting refuses the member's own disclosure (`delegated`). Underwriting refuses an authority that is not in force with `authority`; the request then stays in the outbox and is tried each pass until the authority is activated.
- **Continuing underwritten cover** (setting `continuation.underwritten_excess`, off unless the scheme agreed it). A leaver's offer then also covers the underwritten amount in place when they left. The continuation policy is issued for the automatic part, and underwriting adds the rest as a layer from the policy's first day, on the terms it accepted before and with no new evidence (`group_continuation` proposal, `grp-cont:{scheme}:{offer}`). Its outcome is recorded on the offer.
- **Leave of absence.** A member on leave (an overseas posting, parental leave) keeps cover for the days the terms allow. When the leave starts, `absence_started` is published with the day cover ends if they are not back; past the limit they are taken off cover on that day (`absence_limit_reached`). Each time the module asks the documents module for a letter to the member (`request_letter`, caller `group`, kinds `absence_started` and `absence_limit_reached`, request `grp:{kind}:{scheme}:{life}:{since}`, detail `since` and `cover_ends`; `letter_envelope` in `adapters.rs`). A member not yet linked to a person cannot be written to, and a letter the documents module refuses is not sent again: both raise `letter_not_requested` for staff, naming the request. The wording is the documents area's template.
- **Pools.** A pool scheme has no files: the employer declares headcount and total salary per pool, each declaration replaces the last from its date, and a declaration that is late is raised. While one is overdue the scheme's renewal cannot be agreed (`declaration_overdue`).
- **The scheme year.** Renewal opens at the lead time. Agreed terms take effect on the renewal date. Under a rate guarantee the year rolls on the same terms. With no agreement the scheme runs on old rates for the days allowed, raised to staff, and then ends, or ends at once, as the terms say. Ending a scheme, by staff or by renewal, removes every member on the date (each with a continuation option) and cancels the policy.
- **Money as the employer sees it.** From the core's events the module keeps each invoice by billing group and again by benefit category and occupation class (`by_class`, which adds up to the same total; a scheme whose rates are split by class, such as named directors on a hazardous site, has a line per class, and a mapping names the class column as `occupation_class`), retrospective adjustments against the group they belong to, loadings as one total, and the balance. Each invoice also shows what the scheme still owed from before it (`brought_forward_minor`), so a short payment is carried onto the next invoice as one line for the employer and never against a member. A scheme that pays short as many times in a row as its terms allow takes no new joiners until it has paid; existing members are untouched.
- **The scheme's broker** (`terms.rs` `broker`, `state.rs` `broker_layers`). A scheme placed by a broker names the broker's engagement and book, the commission schedule and option, and the expected annual premium of each cover driven by member data. At registration, or when a broker is first added, the module asks the commission module (`ic-distribution`) to write each scheme cover's layer 0 by that engagement into that book (`write_layer`, caller `group-admin`). A layer the commission module does not know yet (its feed of the core has not reached the scheme) is tried again on a later pass; a refusal a person must put right (broker not authorised, no such book or schedule) raises `broker_not_recorded` for staff. Changing or removing a broker by amending the terms is refused (`broker_change`): it is a dated move of the scheme's book, made in the commission module. A direct scheme names no broker and earns no commission.
- **Refusals leave no trace.** A refused command changes nothing. A repeated command gets its first result, marked as a repeat.

## How it runs

Three parts.

1. **The state machine** (`GroupState`) is hosted on every ledger node in its own replicated group, as module `group` (`ic_group::module_spec()` in `ic_node::modules()`). It reads the core's events: the business date (its daily tick), scheme invoices, adjustments and balances, and underwritten layers. It never reads a clock or the network. Everything it wants done outside is an entry in its outbox, which is part of its replicated state.
2. **The driver** (`driver.rs`, `adapters.rs`) carries the outbox: commands to the core as caller `group-admin` (the core's answer goes back as `CoreResult`, and only then does the entry leave), notices to the reinsurance module, requests and withdrawals to the underwriting module (whose answers it reads back from that module's outbox and records as decisions), joiners to the customer master, and events to the `group-events` hub on Event Hubs. It also takes each stored file one step: record the scan result, or read, map and submit its rows. Every result is a command whose key comes from the work itself, so a driver stopped anywhere carries on when restarted and nothing is done twice. An entry that cannot be delivered stays in the outbox and does not hold up the ones behind it.
3. **The service** (`service.rs`, binary `ic-group`) runs the driver on a timer and serves the routes below. It holds no state: any number can run.

Commands are an `Envelope` (`key`, `caller`, `actor`, `today`, `command`). Callers are fixed names: `group-admin` (staff), `employer-portal`, `member-portal`, `underwriting`, `reinsurance`, `cash-matching`, and `group-driver` for the driver's own. Questions (`host::Question`) cover a scheme's standing, a file with its preview and row results, invoices, open exceptions, and what the driver has to do.

### Routes

Callers are services, each with its own workload identity. A caller presents an Entra ID token issued for this service's application (`IC_GROUP_AUDIENCE`), and the token's app roles are the caller names it may use: `group-admin`, `employer-portal`, `member-portal`, `underwriting`, `reinsurance`, `cash-matching`. A request under a caller name the token does not carry is refused with 403 `role_not_held`; a missing, expired or foreign token is 401; and if Entra ID's signing keys cannot be fetched the answer is 503, because nothing is known about the token. The calling service has established who the person is and names them in `actor`. There is no shared key.

- `POST /v1/schemes/{scheme}/files/{file}` with the file as the body and `x-ic-caller` (`employer-portal` or `group-admin`) and `x-ic-actor`. The bytes are stored in quarantine before the module hears of the file. 202 on success. Limit 32 MB.
- `POST /v1/commands` with `{key, caller, actor, command}`. The business date is the core's. The driver's commands and `receive_file` are not accepted here.
- `POST /v1/questions` with a question. The outbox is not served.
- `POST /v1/schemes/{scheme}/mappings/try` with `{mapping, sample}`, role `group-admin` only: what the sample would do read with the proposed mapping. Nothing is stored.
- `GET /healthz` (the process is up), `GET /readyz` (the cluster answers; the readiness probe) and `GET /metrics` (Prometheus text: `ic_group_passes_total`, `ic_group_passes_failed_total`, `ic_group_steps_total`, and the gauge `ic_group_waiting`, the steps the last pass could not take). None needs a token. Alert on `ic_group_waiting` staying above zero and on failed passes rising.

422 carries the module's reject code. 503 means nothing is known to have happened: send the same request again with the same key.

### On Azure

- **Blob Storage** holds uploaded files in a storage account of their own (`infra/group.bicep`), Entra ID only, deleted after 35 days by a lifecycle rule.
- **Microsoft Defender for Storage** scans each blob on upload and writes the result as the index tag `Malware Scanning scan result`. The driver reads the tag: no tag yet means wait, `No threats found` means clean, and anything else refuses the file. The hash taken at upload is checked again when the file is read.
- **Event Hubs** carries the module's events on `group-events`, one partition, each with `event-id`, `seq`, `module` and `type` properties.
- **Workload identity** for the service (`AzureCredential::from_env`), with Storage Blob Data Contributor on the container and Event Hubs Data Sender on the hub and nothing else.
- **Key Vault** holds the fingerprint salt, mounted into the pod with the cluster token.
- **Entra ID** identifies callers (`auth.rs`). The service's app registration defines one app role per caller name, and each calling service's identity is assigned the roles it needs. Signing keys are fetched from the tenant, kept for an hour, and fetched again when a new key ID appears.

## Operating it

- **Exceptions** (`{"ask":"exceptions"}`) are the work queue for staff: possible matches, held files, refused rows, a blocked scheme, stale files, overdue declarations, persistent short payment, renewal not agreed, and a total of loadings over too few members. Each is published as an event when raised and when cleared.
- **A pass that leaves work waiting** logs a warning with the count. Work waiting for days on one peer shows as the same count every pass; the entries are in the outbox with the peer's name.
- **A scheme is blocked** (`scheme_blocked` with the core's code): fix the cause on the policy (for example reinstate a lapsed scheme), then send `resume`. Nothing was dropped.
- **An employer file removed people in error.** Send `freeze` so the next automatic file is not taken. Send `reinstate` with the employee IDs: each is put back from the day they were removed, with underwritten layers restored and open continuation options withdrawn. Ask the employer for a corrected file, then `unfreeze`.
- **A file is stuck in quarantine.** No scan tag has appeared. Check Defender for Storage on the quarantine account (the monthly scan cap in particular). Nothing reads the file meanwhile; after the scheme's file age limit it is raised as stale. `withdraw_file` frees the scheme for another upload.

## Tests

`cargo test -p ic-group`. The state machine is tested against the real core (`ic-sim`), with a stand-in for the driver that carries commands, answers and events exactly as the driver does: intake, applying, cover above the automatic amount, leavers, the scheme year, billing, administration and the host's seams. The driver is tested with each outside party failing in turn, the Azure adapters against the stand-in that speaks the Blob Storage and Event Hubs protocols, and the whole arrangement on a real node (`tests/cluster.rs`).

## Known gaps

- **Limits of the hand-off to underwriting.** A request reaches the underwriting module as a proposal with entry `group_excess`, named `grp-uw:{scheme}:{case}`, for one life and the excess amount; a request nobody wants any more is withdrawn; accepted terms and endings come back as decisions, carried by underwriting's driver and by this one under the same key (`uw:{effect key}`), so each is recorded once; and when the core has written the layer, or refused it, underwriting is told so that it confirms reinsurance capacity. `tests/underwriting.rs` takes a member through all of it with the real underwriting state machine and its shipped rules (accepted, withdrawn and lapsed), and `tests/cluster.rs` sends the request to that module on a real node. What no test does is run the two service processes and reinsurance's capacity answer together: the capacity answer in that test is given by hand. The proposal carries no bound quote (the premium is the scheme's) and gives the policy's ID as its number; underwriting is told the amount a previous insurer had accepted and reinsurance's reference on the proposal's entry (`takeover_accepted_minor`, `reinsurance_reference`) but not the facultative path; and the member's consent to the employer seeing a loading is recorded in underwriting by a signed step that no portal screen collects yet, so until one does, on a scheme whose terms itemise loadings an accepted answer with a loading arrives without consent, is refused (`consent_required`), logged, and the case stays open. A request for a member not yet linked to a person waits until staff resolve the match.
- **Reinsurance notices are followed as far as its exceptions.** The reinsurance module is hosted under the name `reinsurance` and notices (scheme terms, risk factors, possible matches) are sent to it. `tests/cluster.rs` checks the command shapes against that module's own types and, on a real node hosting both, that a possible match raised from a file appears on reinsurance's list of exceptions and its books still reconcile. No test here checks the retained amounts reinsurance then works out; that is tested in its own crate.
- **Matching depends on what the employer's file carries.** The customer master (`crates/customers`, `http://ic-customers` in the cluster) links only on a strong identifier (IRD number, email or mobile) with a score of 80 or more, so a file with names and birth dates alone creates people or raises possible matches and never links. A mapping can name a mobile phone column; a New Zealand number written for dialling at home is sent with the country code, and a number from elsewhere is used only if the file gives it in international form. A mobile with a name and a birth date scores below the customer master's linking threshold, so on its own it finds the person as a possible match for staff and does not link. `tests/driver.rs` runs the driver against that service itself, over HTTP with a signed token and PostgreSQL, when `IC_TEST_POSTGRES_URL` is set (CI sets it). The group service's identity needs that service's `group-admin` app role in Entra ID, which `infra/group.bicep` does not assign.
- **Loadings billed to the member: what is not finished.** Terms may bill a loading to the employer as one total, to the employer itemised with the member's consent, or to the member; and with a total, a minimum count sends a member's loading to the member when fewer members than the count hold underwritten cover at the time their terms are accepted. The layer is written to the core with the member paying the loading and the standard rate on the scheme invoice, and when the core lapses a member's unpaid cover the case ends here and the employer hears only that it is no longer in place. Who pays is settled when terms are accepted, because it is what the member was told in the offer, and the module never changes it by itself. If leavers or ended cover later take the members with underwritten cover below the count while a loading is still in the employer's total, the exception `loading_total_too_small` is raised for staff (it names no member) and clears when the number recovers or the total is empty. The module does not keep underwriting terms, which are health information, so moving such a loading to the member goes through underwriting: staff ask it to reoffer the terms, the member agrees to pay the loading, and underwriting sends the terms again marked `member_pays_loading`. The module then writes a new layer with the member paying from that day and ends the old one from the same day once the new one is in the core; if the member declines or the offer runs out, `loading_move_declined` is published and nothing changes. `tests/underwriting.rs` runs the whole move with the real underwriting state machine. No staff screen sends the request to underwriting yet. A lapsed layer is not reinstated by itself: when the member asks for the cover back, staff send `request_excess_again` and underwriting decides again, from that day. Giving the member a way to pay and the notices before a lapse belong to the core and the channels and are not built there yet.
- **A continuation policy carries automatic cover only.** The option is for up to the automatic amount at leaving, on the retail product's standard terms. Underwritten excess and its terms are not carried over.
- **Two file layouts per scheme at most.** A scheme has one mapping for full files and may have another for change files (a payroll feed between monthly files). Each is replaced only by a later version of itself. A file is read with the layout it fits, the change layout tried first, and a payroll change goes through the same preview and approval as any file. Staff can try a new mapping version against a sample of the employer's file before setting it (`POST /v1/schemes/{scheme}/mappings/try` with `{mapping, sample}`, role `group-admin`): the answer gives the counts, the refused rows by number and code and whether the safety check would hold the file, and nothing is stored. The trial is a step staff take; setting a mapping does not require one to have passed.
- **Health plan menu: the menu and members' choices are built; putting them into force is not.** Terms `health_menu`: plans (each a set of the product's covers with an excess level), the default plan, the tiers offered (single, couple, family) and how far back a choice may take effect. Each is checked against the scheme's product. A member, the employer or staff send `choose_health_plan {scheme, employee_id, plan, tier, effective}`; the employer's view of a member shows the plan and tier (the default plan, single, until chosen) and never who the dependants are. The core does not yet let a scheme member hold their own set of covers, or carry dependants as lives billed to the member; the core thread proposed the contract on 13 November (`SetMemberCovers {policy, life, covers, effective, reference}` with the payer set per cover, and dependants as lives with `dependant_of`) and group agreed it; health-only schemes come after per-member covers. Until then a choice is recorded and published (`health_plan_chosen`) and nothing is sent to the core, and a scheme whose product has no salary-driven cover cannot be registered.
- **Scheme split (built 13 Nov).** Staff send `split_scheme {scheme, into, new_policy, new_number, employer, effective, employee_ids, terms}` when part of an employer is sold or demerged. Refused before anything is sent when the scheme is not active or is blocked or frozen (`scheme_not_ready`), a file, change or other split is being worked through (`scheme_busy`), either scheme is a pool scheme (`pool_scheme`), the new ID or policy is taken (`scheme_exists`, `invalid_scheme`), the terms are invalid, a member is named twice or not at all (`invalid_split`) or is not a member (`unknown_member`), every member would move (`not_a_split`; a whole scheme is transferred), or underwriting is still deciding cover for a moving member (`excess_open`). The members go to the core as `SplitScheme` in parts of at most one membership chunk; the new scheme is opened here on the first part with its own employer and terms and the old scheme's file layouts, is announced to reinsurance and to the commission module for its broker, and each moved member keeps their life ID, joining date, health choice, absence and accepted underwritten cover (same case and layer). On the old scheme a moved member's record ends on the date; nobody is a leaver, so nothing is offered or underwritten again. No files are taken on the old scheme until the last part is in (`scheme_splitting`). Events: `split_started`, `members_moved` per part, `split_completed`; a part the core refuses raises `split_refused` with the count already moved and a row exception, drops the parts not yet sent, and opens the old scheme to files again. The new scheme's policyholder in the core is the old one's until the core changes it; this module bills and reports to the new employer. Tests: `tests/split.rs`.
- **The app registration is a script, not a template.** `deploy/entra/group-app.sh <name> <group-identity-principal-id> [<role>=<principal-id> ...] [customers=<customer-master-app-id-uri>]` makes the service's app registration with its six app roles (fixed IDs), assigns each named calling service's identity its role, gives the service's own identity the customer master's `group-admin` role, and prints the value for `IC_GROUP_AUDIENCE`. It is safe to run again (`tests/entra_app.rs` runs it against a stand-in Azure CLI). It needs a person with rights to create app registrations, so it is not part of the Bicep deployment. The person is named by the calling service in `actor` and is not verified again here.
- **`infra/group.bicep` has not been deployed.** CI compiles it with the other templates, but `main.bicep` does not reference it yet, so it is deployed on its own into the same resource group, and the Defender for Storage resource uses a preview API version. `deploy/k8s/group.yaml` is in the kustomization and has likewise not been applied to a cluster.
