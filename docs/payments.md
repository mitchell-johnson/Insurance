# Payments and bank channels

`crates/payments` (`ic-payments`) is money in and money out: direct debit authorities, payee accounts, payment and collection runs, refunds, bank statements and their reconciliation, and the evacuation drill. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md` and hosted on the ledger nodes under the name `payments`. `crates/payments-service` (`ic-payments-service`) is everything around it that touches the outside world: the bank channels, the pump that carries submissions to the banks and their answers back, the evidence store, and a fake bank for tests.

No real bank is connected. The channels are built against the interfaces in the design notes and tested against the fake bank in `crates/payments-service/src/fake.rs`. The wire format is the module's own and is replaced per bank when a bank is chosen ("What is not built", below).

## What it does

- **Configuration through the log** (`config.rs`). Banks, channels (file or API, with a cutoff), bank accounts by purpose and statutory fund, routes (primary channel, secondary, urgent, approvals needed, item, run and day limits) and rules. Each channel lists an endpoint per region with its own credential name, signing key name, certificate expiry and egress address. A configuration that leaves any voter region without a working, whitelisted, unexpired endpoint on any channel is refused with `region_not_provisioned` (addendum entry 6).
- **Direct debit authorities** (`authority.rs`). Set up, amended, suspended and cancelled by a payee maintainer. Repeated dishonours suspend an authority. An authority can be marked as the payer being in financial hardship (`mark_hardship`, by collections or a payee maintainer). A dishonour for want of funds on such an authority is not queued to be presented again: collections is asked to call and offer hardship help, and only a recorded conversation (`record_conversation`, with a note) that agrees to it lets the collection be presented once more (COL-016). The marker is set by a person; it is not yet copied from the customer master's vulnerability record. When the core records that a payer has died, the authority is cancelled at once and nothing more is collected under it. An authority can name a scheme member (`life`) who pays for their own cover. What it collects reaches the core as that member's receipt (`ApplyMemberReceipt`) and never as the employer's money; a dishonour reverses it like any other receipt.
- **Payee accounts** (`payee.rs`). A new or changed account is unverified until confirmed with the bank and then cools off for the configured hours before it can be paid. Estate and liquidator payees also need legal sign-off. A blocked account cannot be added under another name.
- **A hold the customer sets** (`hold.rs`, SVC-022, decision 3). The customer master owns a person's own hold on money going out (`docs/customers.md`, "A person's own spending hold"); this module enforces it. Each pass the pump asks the master (`GET /v1/parties/{id}/spending-hold`, settings `IC_CUSTOMERS_URL` and `IC_CUSTOMERS_SCOPE`) about every party in `{"q":"hold_watch"}` (parties with a refund owed, a unit-linked payout waiting, a refund or policy-value payout in an unapproved run, or a hold standing) whose answer is older than half `hold_check_max_age_minutes` (15 minutes when unset), and records it (`record_customer_hold`, service role). While a party is held, a refund or a surrender, withdrawal or loan payout (source `payout:`, `surrender:`, `withdrawal:` or `loan:`) to any payee or authority of that party is refused when it joins a run and held (`customer_hold`) if it was already in one when the run is checked, and a payee cannot be registered for the party nor a payee's account changed. Premium collection and claim payments are not held. When `hold_check_max_age_minutes` is set (1 to 1440), the check also holds such an item (`hold_unconfirmed`) whose party the master has not answered for within that time, so a master that is down stops this money rather than letting it through; a maker checks the run again once the pump has an answer. Question `customer_hold {party}`.
- **Runs** (`run.rs`). A maker prepares a run of one of twelve types, adds items, and checks it. Checks cover payee and authority status, duplicates within the window, limits and available funds. An approver who is not the maker, and who holds no payee or connectivity role, approves it; large runs need two. Approval queues one submission per channel. Items over the pre-advice threshold notify treasury.
- **Submission, exactly once.** A submission is claimed in the module before anything is sent, signed, kept as evidence, and then sent under its own ID. The bank takes an ID once. A send whose outcome is unknown is never repeated: after a grace period the bank is asked whether it holds the batch. A file the bank did not receive is replaced by a new submission with a new ID.
- **A missed window.** A submission a driver takes after the bank's cutoff for its value date (for example because the channel was down through it) goes for the bank's next window instead, skipping weekends and holidays. Its items are recorded as deferred with policy, due date and the date sent for, makers are told, and `{"q":"deferred","from","to"}` lists the population by due date so that any standing or fee the delay caused can be found and reversed (COL-025). Reversing it is the core's and servicing's part: payments charges no fees.
- **Fallback and redirection.** A channel that refuses falls back to the route's secondary channel. A connectivity administrator can redirect a route to the second bank while the first is out.
- **Reading the bank's messages once.** The pump records in the module how far it has read each channel's messages (`note_messages_read`), so a restarted pump carries on from there. The place stops before a message the module did not take, so that message is offered again after a restart.
- **Acknowledgement, settlement and returns.** Each is recorded against its item. Settlement tells the core to settle the payable or receipt the collection; a return reverses it, counts a dishonour, and where the rules allow queues one re-presentment.
- **Payments released by claims** (`intake.rs`). When claims releases a benefit payment (`claim_paid`) or a provider's evidence or rehabilitation cost (`claim_cost_paid`), the event becomes a waiting payment here under the claims payment reference, for the net amount, on the claim's policy. A maker puts waiting payments into a run with `take_waiting`; they are checked and approved like any other item, and settle to the core under the same reference, which is what the core requires. A waiting payment taken out of a run, or whose run is cancelled, waits again. If the bank refuses one, or sends it back before it settles, claims is told that payment failed (`PaymentReturned`, sent to the policy's partition), so the money is owed again there and a task is raised to release it under a new reference. When the last payment of a claims run has settled or failed, claims is given the run's settled total (`ConfirmRun`); once claims has published `run_submitted` with the net total the run released (`submitted_minor`), the confirmation also waits until that much has settled. The payee is the payments payee with that ID, or the one payee of that party; a party with none or several is refused unless the maker names the payee, who must belong to that party.
- **Payments other modules offer** (`intake.rs`). A module's driver offers a payment that is not policy money, such as a reinsurer's net settlement, with `offer_payment` as the `service` role: a reference, a source, the payee or its party, and a positive amount. It waits and is paid like the others; nothing is sent to the core for it, and `item_settled` carries the source back. For a reinsurer settlement (source `reinsurance:statement:<number>`) the pump also tells the reinsurance module, in that module's own commands, that the statement is paid (`record_settlement`, with the bank account it was paid from as `bank_account`) or that the bank refused or returned it (`settlement_returned`); a refusal from that module becomes an exception for a person. Money a reinsurer pays us is matched the same way: a credit on any statement whose reference quotes `ri-stmt-<n>` (any case, anywhere in the reference) is held as unidentified and the reinsurance module is sent `record_settlement` for statement `n` with the amount negated and the statement's account as `bank_account` (key `payments:received:<account>-<date>-<line id>`). When that module records it the exception is resolved; if it refuses (`unknown_statement`, `not_instructed`, a mismatch) the money stays unidentified for a person.
- **Unit-linked payouts** (`intake.rs`). A withdrawal, surrender or price remediation the core owes (`payout_raised`), or owes again under a new reference after a bank return (`payout_reinstructed`), waits the same way under the payout's reference for the exact amount. It is bound to the policyholder the event names: it goes to that party's payee, or to the one the maker names among that party's payees, and to nobody else. The payee's verification, cooling-off and screening are checked by the run as for any other.
- **Refunds** (`refund.rs`). A refund goes back to the account the money came from unless a maker gives a reason and an approver agrees. After the payer's death the source is no longer used: the refund waits for the claim and is paid with it, to whoever the claim pays (addendum entry 1).
- **Cash between statutory funds** (`sweep.rs`, PAY-010). The module remembers which collection account each receipt landed in (a direct debit item, or a direct credit under its cash-matching key `match-<exception>`). When the core says it applied part of that receipt to a policy in another fund (`ReceiptApplied.fund`), the landing account owes that fund the amount; a reversal takes it back, and once the cash has moved a reversal is owed the other way. Question `fund_sweep {as_at}` lists what each account owes, the fund's collection account at the same bank, and what sweep items already carry. A maker moves it with a `fund_settlement` run (which pays from the collection account) holding items with source `fund-sweep:<from account>:<to fund>:<n>` to an `own` payee; the module refuses a sweep that is not that, or that is for more than is open. Settlement clears it and a return reopens it. `fund_cash_settlement` stays red while anything was owed since before its `as_at`. Not built: proposing the sweep run automatically, and member receipts (`MemberReceiptApplied` carries no fund yet).
- **Lists for the staff console** (`console.rs`). `open_runs` (runs prepared, checked or approved, by value date: type, fund, status, makers, approvers, item and held counts, live total), `open_exceptions` (unresolved, oldest first, optional `account`), and `party {party}` (its payees with what blocks each, its authorities, and the customer master's last answer on its hold). None of them carries an account number or name. `next_value_date {run_type, at?}` is the first value date a run of that type can go for at `at` (New Zealand time), through the channel it would use, skipping weekends and holidays (PAY-014). `expected_payment {payment}` is a payment's status and the date it is expected: the run's value date, or the later date it went for after missing its window; `waiting` with no date before it is in a run.
- **Reconciliation** (`recon.rs`). Statement lines are matched to what was expected. Anything else becomes an exception that a reconciler takes and resolves with a reason.
- **The evacuation drill** (`drill.rs`). From each region, a token payment through every channel, signed and sent exactly as a real one is. The `readiness` question says, per region, whether a drill passed within the configured age.
- **Fail-back waits for submissions.** The module answers `{"failback_quiet": true}` with `false` while any submission is being sent, so leadership is not moved under a file in flight.
- **Refusals leave no trace.** A refused command changes nothing; a repeated key gets its first result.

## How it talks to the rest

In: an `Envelope` with `key` (the idempotency key), `actor` (`id` and `roles`), `at` (New Zealand wall-clock time) and `command`. Roles are `maker`, `approver`, `payee_maintainer`, `connectivity_admin`, `legal`, `reconciler`, `treasury`, `service` and `collections`. The caller authenticates the person and states the roles; the module enforces who may do what, including that an approver holds no payee or connectivity role.

Other modules ask for money to move with two commands, both as a `maker`:

- `add_run_items` on a run they prepared with `prepare_run`: each item names its counterparty (a payee or an authority), an amount, and a `source` of the module's choosing that comes back on every event about the item.
- `request_refund` for a premium refund on a policy.

Out, on each applied command: events with a hash-chained sequence (question `trail`), and an outbox of instructions the pump works through in order. `item_settled` and `item_returned` carry the item's `source`; that is how claims, commission and reinsurance learn what happened to a payment they asked for. Instructions to the core settle or return a policy payable, raise or release a refund payable, or ask for a receipt to be matched.

Questions (`{"q": "..."}`): `waiting`, `config`, `run`, `submission`, `batch`, `queued_submissions`, `unconfirmed_submissions`, `submission_in_progress`, `control_totals`, `authority`, `payee`, `refund`, `represent`, `reconciliation`, `readiness`, `outbox` and `trail`.

For the financial close (`docs/finance.md`), both with `as_at`: `fund_cash_settlement` answers `{green, items, count}`, green when no reconciliation break opened by that day is still open and no run valued by that day is still waiting for the bank's settlement; `aged_unidentified_receipts` answers `{as_at, items, total_minor, count}` with unidentified receipts oldest first and who owns each. Both list at most 200 items; the count and total cover everything.

## Running it

The module needs nothing: it is in the node binary. The pump is `ic-payments-pump`, deployed by `deploy/k8s/payments.yaml`, two replicas in each voter region.

1. **Platform.** `infra/main.bicep` creates the pump's identity, a payments vault that only that identity can use, and the immutable `payments-evidence` container. Fill the `REPLACE-` values in `deploy/k8s/payments.yaml` from the outputs `paymentsClientId`, `paymentsKeyVaultName`, `paymentsKeyVaultUrl`, `keyVaultName`, `blobEndpoint` and `tenantId`, and set `IC_REGION` to the region's name as the payments configuration will name it.
2. **Per bank channel and region**, as the connectivity administrator: create the signing key in the payments vault (`az keyvault key create --vault-name <paymentsKeyVaultName> --name <channel>-<region>-signing --kty EC-HSM --curve P-256 --ops sign verify`), store the credential the bank issued as a secret, give the bank the public key and the region's egress address to whitelist, and list the secret in the `ic-payments-bank-credentials` class.
3. **Activate the configuration** with `activate_config`. It is refused until every voter region is provisioned on every channel.
4. **Run a drill from each region** (`kubectl create job --from=cronjob/ic-payments-drill first-drill`) before the first real run.

Settings: `IC_REGION`, `IC_BANK_SECRETS_DIR`, `IC_KEY_VAULT_URL`, `IC_BLOB_ENDPOINT`, `IC_PAYMENTS_CONTAINER` (default `payments-evidence`), `IC_PUMP_INTERVAL_SECS` (15), `IC_PUMP_GRACE_MINUTES` (10, at least 2), and the cluster settings every client takes. The pump does not start without a vault, an evidence store and its credentials directory: there is no unsigned or unrecorded mode.

## Runbook

- **A submission shows `failed`.** The bank refused it or did not receive it. Read the reason on the submission. Never send the same file again by hand. A maker issues `resubmit`, which makes a new submission with a new ID; the old one stays on record as failed.
- **A submission stays `sending`.** The pump lost the connection mid-send. After the grace period it asks the bank whether it holds the batch and records the answer. If the bank cannot be asked, it stays `sending` and blocks fail-back; when the bank confirms by another route, the service role records `record_submitted` or `record_not_received`.
- **A bank is out.** The pump logs `not done this pass` with the channel. Where the route has a secondary channel it is used without anyone acting. To move a route to the second bank for the duration, a connectivity administrator issues `redirect_route` for the run type with `secondary: true`, and again with `secondary: false` to end it.
- **The drill job failed.** The job's log names the channel. Check, for that region: the certificate expiry, that the bank still whitelists the egress address, and that the credential secret is current. Readiness for the region stays stale until a drill passes.
- **A region is lost.** Pumps in the surviving regions carry on with their own endpoints, keys and credentials. Nothing is reconfigured. Submissions the lost region had claimed and not confirmed are asked about after the grace period by whichever pump is left.
- **A problem instruction.** If the core refuses an instruction, the action is marked failed and a reconciliation exception is raised for a person. If the core cannot be reached, the outbox waits in order.
- **Proving what was sent.** `payments-evidence/payments/<submission>/file.txt` and `manifest.json` hold the exact bytes and the signed manifest. The submission in the module holds the same content hash.

## What is not built

- **A real bank's transport, and reading ISO 20022 answers.** Each channel has a `format`: the system's own (default) or `iso20022`, chosen for real banks on 13 November 2026. An ISO 20022 channel is sent `pain.001.001.09` or `pain.008.001.08`; that XML is what is hashed, signed and kept as evidence (`payments/<submission>/file.xml`), and every account at that bank must carry `at_bank` (holder, number, BIC). Run IDs (31 characters) and item IDs (35) are held to what ISO 20022 can carry when they are made. The bank's answers are read the same way (`iso20022::read_messages`): a `pain.002` status report becomes the acknowledgement, a `camt.054` notification the settlements and returns it reports, and a `camt.053` statement those and then the statement, with a batch's line under its payment information ID and a return's under the item's end-to-end reference. The channel's messages are then `[{seq, document}]`; each part of a document is recorded under its own key, and the pump's place stops before a document any part of which the module refused. A document that cannot be read is reported every pass and nothing after it is read. Each endpoint can name a client certificate (`client_certificate`, a secret holding the certificate and key in PEM) and the bank's private authority (`bank_authority`); `HttpBank` presents the one and trusts the other, and an ISO 20022 channel cannot be activated with a region that presents no client certificate (`region_not_provisioned`). A missing secret stops the channel for that region with the secret's name. Because the pump asks the bank what it holds before taking a submission, a bank that turns our certificate away never has a submission taken. SFTP is not built: the design notes ask for a secure transfer with a signed manifest, which HTTPS with mutual TLS is. If the chosen bank offers only SFTP, it is a second `Bank` implementation beside `HttpBank`.
- **Drill payments on the statement.** A drill's token payments arrive on the bank statement as lines the module does not expect, and are resolved as exceptions by a reconciler. They are not posted to the ledger.
- **Postings for non-policy payments.** Policy payables and collections are posted in the core. Commission, reinsurer and supplier payments are signalled by `item_settled` and `item_returned` to the module that asked; the ledger entries for them belong to those modules and finance.
- **Account details are held in the module's state** in clear, as the bank needs them. They are not sealed per person, so they cannot be destroyed separately from the log.
- **Notices go to the structured log** (`payments.notice`) with their role. Delivery to a work queue or to people is the channels and documents modules' part and is not wired.
- **Claims payments: what is left.** A unit-linked payout the bank rejects before settlement is not reported to the core; its reference is spent and servicing must instruct it again. A `claim_paid` event whose amount or policy cannot be read is refused and logged by the module host, not raised as a task. A payment goes only to the account claims verified: a release whose `account_ref` is neither blank, the payee's ID, nor the payee's account number (letters and digits only compared) is refused when taken into a run (`account_mismatch`). Payments does not send claims `RecordRepayment`: an inbound credit is not tied to an overpayment.
- **Scheme members: what is left.** Collection from a member is by the member's own authority only. An incoming credit a member pays in themselves is not matched to the member, because no payment reference is issued per member. `PaymentAuthorityCancelled` names a policy and no life, so on a scheme policy it ends every authority on the policy, the employer's included. Member credit refunds (`RefundMemberCredit`) are settled by the core and are not paid through a run.
- **Receipt matching.** A `match` instruction hands an inbound credit to the core's cash matching with the bank reference; resolving the payer from an authority before matching is not done.
- **Egress addresses are recorded, not enforced.** The configuration records the address each region presents to the bank. Giving each region's pump a fixed egress address is the network's job when the second region is provisioned.
