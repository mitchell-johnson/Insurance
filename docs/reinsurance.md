# Reinsurance

`crates/reinsurance` (`ic-reinsurance`) is the module that knows what the insurer holds on each life across every policy and partition, works out what is ceded on each cover, and keeps each reinsurer's account. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md`. Treaties are in `treaties/`.

## What it does

- **Treaties as code.** A treaty is a Starlark file loaded into typed terms (`treaty.rs`, `loader.rs`): quota share, surplus or excess of loss per life, the panel and its shares, how the reinsurer is paid (its own rate table, or a proportion of the customer's premium), commission, claim notification and consent thresholds, group terms, statement period, profit commission, what a listing may say about a life, and the clauses a disputed item cites. Every treaty ships worked scenarios and they run at load: a treaty that does not do what its file says does not load. Versions are activated through the log and are immutable once active. Cover in force stays on the version it was written under until a recapture or a negotiated move changes it by command. A reinsurer's rate table that rates by sex prices a life whose sex it does not hold (recorded without a rating sex, as for a life who is neither male nor female) at the lower of its female and male rates for the life's other facts, or at a stated blend (`reinsurer_rate_table(..., unmatched_sex = sex_blended("0.5"))`): the basis of `docs/decisions.md` decision 1. A life with no recorded facts is still never priced by guess.
- **Per-life accumulation.** The module reads every partition's event stream (`feed.rs`), de-duplicates on the sequence and refuses a batch with a gap. Covers are held per life: a life linked to a party accumulates across all policies and partitions; a scheme member not linked to a party accumulates only against their own record. Treaties that name the same `retention_group` share one retention per life.
- **The cession calculation** (`cession.rs`) is a pure function of the covers on one life and their treaty versions. Retention is filled in a fixed order, by start date and then by key, so the answer never depends on the order events arrived in. The result is a timeline per cover. A backdated change is the same calculation with one more date in it, and only what changed is sent on, from the date it changed.
- **Never silently over-retained.** Cover over the retention that no treaty or facultative terms take is recorded as unplaced and is always an open exception. For scheme cover written by a membership file, the excess on a known life is ceded facultatively under the treaty's standing terms for group business, or flagged to group administration for the underwritten-excess path, as the scheme's terms say. A possible match between a member and a party is an exception and is shown to an underwriter, but moves nobody's retention until a person confirms it.
- **The capacity step** (`capacity.rs`) answers, before terms are offered, what is held on a life and whether the treaty takes a new cover automatically. Capacity is reserved for the life of the proposal so that two proposals on one life see each other. Facultative terms are recorded against the reservation and follow it to the issued layer.
  A treaty's `exclusions` are underwriting's cause tags for what was declared or assessed: hazardous pursuits (`pursuit.aviation`), occupation class (`occupation.class_4`) and travel (`travel.*`), never anything from the health questions. Underwriting sends the life's tags as `risk_codes` on the capacity request. A code ending in `.*` excludes its whole family; a treaty naming anything that is not such a tag is refused when loaded. Any match makes the request excluded, needing facultative terms.
- **Ceded premium** (`premium.rs`) is worked out per billing period from the cession timeline and posted as differences, each with the period's own value date. When the core reverses a period's billing in an unwind, the ceded premium for it reverses the same way. A cover that cannot be priced is charged nothing and raised as an exception; a rate is never guessed.
- **Recoveries** (`claims.rs`) follow the cession at the insured event. The expected recovery is posted as recoverable and becomes due from the reinsurer as the claim is paid. Large claims are notified to the panel, and consent is tracked where the treaty gives reinsurers a say. Catastrophe and stop loss covers respond to what the insurer paid and kept across claims.
- **Disputed items.** When an admission is reversed, recoveries already taken are not reversed. Each becomes a disputed item on the reinsurer's account citing the treaty's errors-and-omissions and claims-cooperation clauses, and is settled by two people for nothing, part or all.
- **Statements, listings and settlement** (`account.rs`). A statement takes everything on a reinsurer's account valued up to the period end and nets it. Its listing carries the policy reference, dates, sums and only the facts the treaty allows; every release is logged. A cession that changes for a period already listed flags the statement, blocks its settlement, and is corrected by a supplementary listing that shows each row as sent and as it is now. Settlement needs the reinsurer's agreement where the treaty requires it, a maker and a checker, and no hold; it produces one sweep per partition and one net payment.
- **Reconciliation and comparison** (`views.rs`). `reconcile` rebuilds every cession timeline and checks the accounts against the periods and claims behind them. There are views for exposure by reinsurer, concentration by scheme and site, the migration comparison with legacy retention records, and what a proposed treaty version would change across the in-force book.

## How it talks to the rest

In: `Command::CoreEvents` from the event feed; capacity commands from underwriting and new business; scheme terms and possible matches from group administration; claim facts from claims; statement and settlement commands from operations and month-end.

Out, on each applied command: `Instruction`s for the core (record this cession, post this entry, sweep this balance), each with a stable ID that is its idempotency key there, and `Notice`s for other modules and people (exceptions, underwritten-excess requests, large-claim notifications, consent requests, disputed items, documents to file in write-once Blob Storage, settlements due).

## Reaching the core

The core keeps two things for reinsurance, both by command from caller `reinsurance` and neither worked out there.

- **The cession on a cover.** `record_cession` replaces a cover's record from a date with dated segments, kept on the policy (`Policy.cessions`, read with `ic_core::reinsurance::cession_on`). The core checks only that the parts of each cession add up to its sum at risk and that the dates are in order. The record moves with the policy when it transfers between partitions.
- **Reinsurance entries.** `post_reinsurance` posts one balanced entry of a named kind: ceded premium, commission, a recoverable, a recovery due, an aggregate recovery, profit commission, a dispute settlement, an opening balance or a settlement sweep. A negative amount posts the opposite entry. Each reinsurer has one account per treaty and partition, `int/reinsurer/{treaty}/{reinsurer}/{currency}`; the sum across partitions is the balance the module holds, and the end-to-end tests assert it.

`Reinsurance::to_core` turns an instruction into its command, with the instruction's ID as the idempotency key. The driver's rules are short. An instruction the core accepts, or reports as a duplicate, is done. A cession the core refuses with `policy_transferring` or `policy_transferred` is dropped: the core keeps the refusal against the key, so a retry would not apply, and the module sends the cover's whole record again when the transfer lands or is abandoned. Entries are never refused for a transfer, because premium already billed is owed from the partition that billed it. Any other refusal is a defect and stops the driver for a person to look at.

## Hosting and the driver

The module is hosted on the ledger nodes as the group `reinsurance` (`host.rs`, listed in `ic_node::modules()`). The host shows it every partition's events; each becomes a feed batch keyed by the event's ID. `host::Query` lists the questions it answers: the outbox, a cover's cession, what is retained on a life, a reinsurer's balance, a statement, a listing, exposure, scheme concentration, exceptions and its own reconciliation.

**Claims.** The claims module lives in the core and publishes its facts on each partition's event feed (`Event::Module { module: "claims", .. }`). `claims::fact_from_event` reads the kinds reinsurance uses: `claim_reserved`, `claim_reserve_changed`, `claim_admitted` (ignored for a benefit in payment, which is admitted with no sum), `claim_paid`, `payment_returned` (a payment taken back off, less any interest for late payment, which is the insurer's own cost and is not ceded; a returned provider fee, marked `cost`, is ignored), `claim_closed`, `claim_reopened`, `admission_reversed` and `overpayment_written_off`. A fact that cannot be read or taken never refuses the feed: it opens the exception `claim_fact:{claim}`, which closes when operations enter the fact with `Command::Claim` (callers `claims` and `reinsurance-ops`). In the other direction the driver sends each notice for claims to the partition holding the claim's policy as `Command::Claims(Reinsurance(..))` from caller `reinsurance`: large-claim notification, consent required, consent, disputed item raised and dispute resolved (`Query::NoticeDelivery` gives the command; `tests/claims.rs` pins the wire format and `tests/driver.rs` runs it against the real claims module). A notice the core refuses is set aside with the exception `notice:{n}`; `retry_notice` sends it again under a new key, because the core answers a key it refused with the same refusal.

`Query::SweepCheck { period_end }` (`{"sweep_check": {"period_end": "2027-03-31"}}`) is the close check finance reports as `reinsurance_sweep`. It answers `{"green": bool, "items": [...]}`. Each item has a `kind`: `statement_not_cut` (a treaty and reinsurer with entries valued by the date, a statement period ending on it, and no statement to it), `supplementary_listing_due` (a statement to the date or earlier whose listing changed after it was sent), `instruction_refused` and `instructions_not_delivered` (the core has not posted everything, so its reinsurer accounts do not yet net to the statements), and `does_not_reconcile`. A statement period ends by the calendar: every month end for a monthly treaty, the end of March, June, September and December for a quarterly one, the end of December for a yearly one. A reinsurer's agreement and payment are not part of the check, because they wait on the reinsurer.

Everything the module issues is numbered and kept in its replicated state (`outbox.rs`) until acknowledged, because a command proposed by the event feed has nobody waiting for its reply. `ic-reinsurance` is the driver process (`driver.rs`, deployed by `deploy/k8s/reinsurance.yaml`). Each pass reads the oldest instructions, sends each to its partition in order, and acknowledges what the core took. It holds no state, and two may run at once.

- A pass cut short by a lost node is finished by the next. The core applies an instruction once.
- A cession refused because its policy is in transfer is passed over, as described above.
- Instructions for a partition with no business date yet wait.
- Any other refusal holds that instruction's lane and opens the exception `delivery:{n}` with the core's code and message. It stays stopped until the core takes the instruction, or operations abandon it with `abandon_instruction` (a reason and an approver, kept in the state and answered by `Query::Abandoned`). An abandoned posting leaves the module's balance and the ledgers apart by its amount until someone posts the other side.
- A lane is what must reach the core in order (`Instruction::lane`): each cover's cession record is one, since a later record replaces an earlier one from its date, and the money postings, sweeps and clearings on one partition for one treaty and reinsurer are another, since they move that pair's account. Instructions in other lanes are delivered past a held one and acknowledged by number (`acknowledge_delivered`); the held one is offered again on every pass.

After instructions, each cycle hands notices on to the audience each is for, with the notice's outbox number in the idempotency key. A notice its audience refuses is set aside with the exception `notice:{n}` and the ones behind it carry on; operations send it again (`retry_notice`) or abandon it with a reason and an approver (`abandon_notice`). An audience that cannot be reached keeps only its own notices waiting: the round carries on with the other audiences and reports the failure.

| Audience | Notices | Where they go |
|---|---|---|
| Group administration | underwritten excess required | the `group` module on the nodes |
| Underwriting | a reinsurer's answer to a facultative referral | the `underwriting` module |
| Payments | settlement due | the `payments` module (see Settlements) |
| Documents | a statement's covering letter | the `documents` module, as a requested letter (see Letters) |
| Claims | large claim, consent, disputed items | the core, as claims commands (see Claims) |
| Records | statements, listings, supplementary listings, the release log | write-once Blob Storage |
| Staff | exceptions opened and closed, supplementary listing due, a capacity hold that lapsed | the module's event hub, and the driver's log |

**Who runs the accounts.** People do, from the portal's reinsurance console (`/reinsurance`, Accounts view; `docs/channels.md`): cutting, agreeing, holding and releasing a statement, supplementary listings, profit commission reviews, and a settlement prepared by one person and instructed when a second signs. Nothing cuts statements on a timer; the sweep check reports a statement not cut at month end (added 15 Nov by the production readiness thread).

**Records** (`carriers.rs`). Each statement and listing is written to the container `reinsurance-records` under the name the module gave it (`reinsurance/<treaty>/<reinsurer>/statement-<n>.json`), byte for byte; its SHA-256 is in the module's state with the statement. Each release of information to a reinsurer is written as `reinsurance/releases/<notice number>.json` with what was released, to whom, why, and the date it was filed. The container has a time-based retention policy (`infra/reinsurance.bicep`), and the driver only ever creates: a name that exists is read back, accepted if it holds the same thing, and refused as `record_conflict` for a person if it does not. A document that does not have the hash it was issued with is refused as `record_damaged` and not written. While storage is away the notices wait.

**Reinsurers.** Once filed, each statement, listing part and supplementary listing is also left for its reinsurer to collect over SFTP: in the storage account `IC_REINSURANCE_DROPS_ENDPOINT` names, container `ri-<reinsurer>` (the reinsurer ID in lower case, underscores as hyphens), as `<treaty>/<file>`. Each reinsurer is an SFTP local user with read and list on its own container only, by SSH key (`sftpReinsurers` in `infra/reinsurance.bicep`); the driver writes with its identity and no shared keys are enabled. Written once, like the records: a repeat is accepted, other contents under the same name are refused (`record_conflict`), and a reinsurer ID that cannot name a container is refused (`no_drop_for_reinsurer`). The release log never leaves. Without the setting nothing is left for reinsurers, and statements are only filed.

**Reinsurer API.** Reinsurers can also read their statements and listings over HTTPS (`src/reinsurer_api.rs`), served by the driver process when `IC_REINSURANCE_API_LISTEN` is set, behind the ingress controller (`deploy/k8s/reinsurance.yaml`: Service `ic-reinsurance-api`, port 8096, reachable only from the ingress namespace). Each reinsurer has its own Entra app registration in the insurer's tenant, holding a certificate the reinsurer keeps (made by `deploy/entra/apps.sh reinsurer <name> <reinsurer> <certificate.pem>`, which prints its `IC_REINSURANCE_API_CLIENTS` entry), and is granted the app role `Reinsurer.Statements.Read` on the API's own registration (`IC_REINSURANCE_API_AUDIENCE`, access tokens version 2). It calls with a client-credentials token; the token's client (`azp`, or `appid`) is looked up in `IC_REINSURANCE_API_CLIENTS` (`<client id>=<reinsurer>,…`) and is the only thing that says which reinsurer is asking. `GET /v1/statements?after=&limit=` lists that reinsurer's statements (number, treaty, period, currency, net, status, file, hash, supplementary listings; `next_after` when a page is full), from the module question `{"statements": {"reinsurer", "after", "limit"}}`. `GET /v1/files/{treaty}/{file}` returns a `statement-*.json` file from the write-once records under `reinsurance/<treaty>/<that reinsurer>/`, with `x-content-sha256`; the statement names its listing parts. Anything else is not found. No token or a bad one is 401, a client that is not a configured reinsurer or lacks the role is 403, an unreachable module, store or tenant is 503. Every request is logged under target `reinsurance.api` with the reinsurer, client, what was asked and the status, never the content. Rate limits and IP allow-lists, if wanted, belong at the ingress.

**Letters.** A reinsurer named on the treaty panel with its organisation party (`reinsurer(id, share, party = "<party id>")`) is sent a covering letter for each statement: the module raises `StatementLetterDue` and the driver asks the documents module for it with `request_letter` (kind `reinsurance_statement`, request `ri:<statement>`, key `ri-letter:<statement>`), carrying the period, the net, the payment reference and the filed record's name and hash. Documents issues it by email with proof of delivery; its status is the documents question `{"letter_request": {"request": "ri:<n>"}}`. The letter carries totals only: the listing is collected over SFTP or the reinsurer API. A reinsurer with no party on the panel gets no letter.

**Staff.** Each notice is sent to partition 0 of the event hub `reinsurance-events` as `{"seq", "on", "notice"}` with the properties `event-id` (`reinsurance-notice-<seq>`, the same on every try, so a consumer drops a repeat) and `kind`. The same notice is written to the driver's log at warning level under the target `reinsurance.notice`, where an alert rule can read it. The hub keeps seven days; the open exceptions themselves stay in the module (`Query::Exceptions`) whatever happens to the hub. People work them on the staff portal's reinsurance console, which reads the module and not the hub.

Settings: `IC_NODES` and the cluster token as for every process; `IC_BLOB_ENDPOINT` and `IC_EVENT_HUBS_ENDPOINT` (both required: the driver does not start with nowhere to file statements or nobody to tell); and optionally `IC_REINSURANCE_CYCLE_MS` (2000), `IC_REINSURANCE_PATIENCE_MS` (10000), `IC_REINSURANCE_BATCH` (100), `IC_REINSURANCE_RECORDS_CONTAINER` (`reinsurance-records`) and `IC_REINSURANCE_EVENT_HUB` (`reinsurance-events`). It signs in to Azure with its pod's workload identity, which `infra/reinsurance.bicep` creates with three rights: write to that container, send to that hub, read the cluster token. `tests/cluster.rs` runs the hosted module, the host's feed and the shipped binary against a real node and a stand-in for Azure; `tests/carriers.rs` covers the records and the staff feed.

## A change of underwriting terms

When underwriting alters the loadings or exclusions on cover in force, the core ends each affected layer and writes it again from the same day, and the new layer's `LayerAdded` event names the one it `continues`. Nothing about the size of the risk has changed, so the new layer takes over the old one's cession as it stands (`feed.rs`, `continue_layer`): the treaty version it was ceded under, its place in the life's retention order, the date its years toward recapture and its listing run from, its facultative terms and a migrated cession. Cover reinstated on altered terms arrives the same way. A layer said to continue one the module never saw is ceded as new cover.

A facultative reinsurer accepted the risk on the old terms. Where the old layer had facultative terms, the exception `facultative_terms_altered:<policy>/<cover>/<layer>/<life>` asks reinsurance operations to tell the reinsurer; the cession carries on meanwhile. `facultative_reinsurer_told` (caller `reinsurance-ops`, the cover's key and where the reinsurer's answer is filed) closes it. A part reduction is different: the smaller layer the core writes is new cover on purpose, because what is ceded changes.

## Settlements

When a statement is sent for payment (`instruct_settlement`, maker and checker) the driver offers a net due to the reinsurer to the payments module as a released payment: reference `ri-stmt-<statement>`, source `reinsurance:statement:<statement>`, payee the reinsurer's ID, under the key `reinsurance:settlement:<statement>`. The reference and key depend only on the statement number, so however often the offer is repeated it is one payment. It waits in payments until a maker there takes it into a reinsurer run, which needs the reinsurer set up as a verified payee whose payee ID or party is the reinsurer's ID here. If payments refuses the offer the notice is set aside with an exception, like any refused notice.

The payments module's driver tells this module what the bank did with the payment (caller `payments`, keys `payments:settled:<payment>` and `payments:returned:<payment>:<date>`), including an item the bank refuses before it settles; finance and reinsurance operations may record the same by hand for money that moved some other way. `record_settlement` with the statement's net amount marks it settled; any other amount opens `settlement_mismatch:<statement>` and leaves it owed. `settlement_returned` for the payment that settled it makes the statement owed again and opens `settlement_returned:<statement>`, which the next matching settlement closes. Each statement document names its `payment_reference` (`ri-stmt-<statement>`), which a reinsurer paying what it owes is asked to quote. `amount_minor` on `record_settlement` is in the statement's sign: positive paid to the reinsurer, negative received from it. With `bank_account` (the ledger's code for the bank account the money moved through) the module clears each partition's part of settlement clearing against that bank account (`InstructionBody::ClearSettlement`, the core's `settlement_cleared` entry), and a later return puts it back. Recorded without one, as for money moved some other way, it opens `settlement_not_cleared:<statement>` until finance or reinsurance operations name the account with `{"clear_settlement": {"number": <statement>, "bank_account": "<code>"}}`.

A returned settlement is not paid again by itself, because the bank details that bounced must be put right in payments first. Once they are, reinsurance operations send `{"reoffer_settlement": {"number": <statement>, "reference": "<where the decision is filed>"}}` (caller `reinsurance-ops`). The module records the decision on the statement (`reoffers`), closes the exception and offers the payment again as a new payment, since payments never reuses a reference: the n-th offer again has reference `ri-stmt-<statement>-<n + 1>` and key `reinsurance:settlement:<statement>:<n + 1>`, with the same source `reinsurance:statement:<statement>`, so payments' settle and return signals still find the statement. Refusals: `not_returned` (no return waiting, or it was already offered again), `nothing_to_pay` (the reinsurer owes the money), `reference_required`.

## Known gaps

- **The AIA NZ range.** The shipped synthetic retail treaty takes lump sum cover on a life (life, trauma, TPD) on `aia-living`, `aia-living-business`, `aia-cover-for-life`, `aia-essentials-life`, `aia-starter` and `aia-interim`; the group treaty takes `aia-corporate` group life (test `the_aia_range_is_covered_by_the_shipped_treaties`). Income protection, health and accidental injury are retained: no shipped treaty names them, and a real income or health treaty would be negotiated with its own terms.
- **Alerts mail whoever `alertEmails` names.** `infra/reinsurance.bicep` raises a log alert for each exception the module opens (by code, every five minutes) and one when delivery has failed three times in fifteen minutes, on the driver's log in the Log Analytics workspace. Who is on call is not decided, so with no addresses the alerts show in Azure Monitor and tell nobody. Staff work the exceptions on the portal's reinsurance console (`/reinsurance` in `ic-portal`). Nothing reads the event hub yet; the alerts use the log.
- **`infra/reinsurance.bicep` has not been deployed.** CI compiles it with the other templates. Like the other modules' templates it is deployed on its own into the resource group `main.bicep` created, and the retention policy on the records container is created unlocked; locking it in production is a deliberate step for an operator.
- **Statements are filed, not sent.** The filed document is what goes to the reinsurer; sending it (by email, a portal or the reinsurer's bordereau upload) is done by people.
- **A module added to a running cluster** starts at the oldest event each partition still holds and raises `events_not_seen` for that partition. Loading the earlier cover from Event Hubs or the cold tier is not built, here or in the host. Hosted from a cluster's first start, as it is now, it misses nothing.
- **Settlements: what is not automatic.** Re-presenting a returned settlement waits for a person (`reoffer_settlement`), and a settlement recorded without its bank account waits for one to be named (`clear_settlement`). Everything else, from the sweep to clearing and the bank, is posted by the module.
- **Joint lives.** The design counts joint cover in full against each life's retention. The core has no joint-life cover yet, so there is nothing to count.
- **Longevity reinsurance for annuities** is not built: the core has no annuities.
- **Ceded premium is posted by its own entry**, not as lines in the billing entry as the capacity model in the design notes assumes. The module is the only place that knows the cession, and it runs beside the core, so the posting follows the billing event.
- **Original premium, gross or net.** The core's billing event carries the premium by cover type after discounts. A treaty priced on the original premium before discounts is priced on the same figure until the core sends both.
- **Listings are cut in one command.** Cutting a statement walks the whole ceded book. The listing is filed in parts of at most the treaty's `listing_part_rows` (2,000 unless the treaty says otherwise), each its own record named on the statement with its hash, so no one document grows with the book; the walk itself would need splitting by partition for a book of several hundred thousand covers.
- **Ceded reserves** are an actuarial figure and are not held here; the exposure view shows balances, open recoverables, disputed items and ceded sums at risk.
- **Stop loss after catastrophe cover** subtracts the recoveries of catastrophe treaties whose period starts inside the stop loss period. A treaty with a different order of cover needs that said in its terms first.

## A claim reopened after a reversed admission

When claims reopens a claim whose admission was reversed, the module does not start billing again on its own: the reinsurers hold disputed items for what they were asked to repay. It opens the exception `reopened_after_reversal:<claim>` and leaves the claim reversed. Once every disputed item on the claim is resolved, a person in reinsurance operations sends `{"resume_recoveries": {"claim": "<id>", "reference": "<their note>"}}` (caller `reinsurance-ops`). The command is refused with `not_reopened` when no such exception is open, `reference_required` when the reference is blank, and `disputes_open` while an item is unresolved. On success the claim is open again, the exception closes, and recoveries are worked out afresh: what each reinsurer repaid on the reversal is added back to what may be billed, so a reinsurer that repaid is billed again in full and one whose item was written off is billed only the difference. A second reversal of the same claim raises items with the reference of the first followed by `/2`, `/3` and so on, for the amount due less what was already repaid.

## A scheme split

The core's `Event::SchemeSplit` moves named members to a new scheme. Each of their covers on the old scheme ends on the split date and carries on under the new scheme from it with the same cession, treaty version, facultative placement and place in the life's retention order (`feed.rs split_scheme`, test `members_moved_by_a_scheme_split_keep_their_cession`). The new scheme takes the old one's terms until group sets its own. The reinsurer's listing shows the cover under the new scheme's policy number from that date.

## A policy avoided for non-disclosure

**Replacement cover.** When the core announces that new cover replaces cover on another policy on the same life (`CoverReplaced`, after the new layers are issued), the module carries the replaced cession on: the new layers, in layer order up to the amount replaced, take the treaty version the replaced cover was ceded on and its place and age in the life's retention order, as a rewritten layer does. Cover above the amount replaced is new business on the treaty in force. A layer that crosses the amount cannot be split, so it stays new business and the exception `replacement_part:<key>` asks a person to place the replaced part; a replacement of cover never ceded here opens `replacement_unknown:<policy>/<cover>`. The same announcement twice changes nothing. The old policy's cover ends when the core cancels it, and the entries that follow post only the difference.

The core's `PolicyAvoided` event means the policy is treated as never having been in force. The module ends each of its covers on the day the cover started (`feed.rs`), so the cession record holds nothing for the policy on any date. Three things follow from the rebuilt record, each by entries that post only the difference: the retention the policy used on the life goes to the person's other policies from the day each started, and their cessions and ceded premium are corrected; every premium and commission ceded for the avoided policy is taken back, whether the customer's premium was refunded or kept; and recoveries on its claims are worked out again to nothing, so what was billed to a reinsurer is credited back on its account. The event delivered twice changes nothing.

Two things are judgement and worth a reinsurance manager's eye. Recoveries already billed are taken back on the account directly, without a disputed item, because a reinsurer that was never on risk has nothing to dispute. And the reinsurers get their premium back in full even when the insurer keeps the customer's premium; a treaty that lets the reinsurer share retained premium is not modelled.
