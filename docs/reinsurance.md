# Reinsurance

`crates/reinsurance` (`ic-reinsurance`) is the module that knows what the insurer holds on each life across every policy and partition, works out what is ceded on each cover, and keeps each reinsurer's account. It is a deterministic state machine beside the core, built to the contract in `docs/modules.md`. Treaties are in `treaties/`.

## What it does

- **Treaties as code.** A treaty is a Starlark file loaded into typed terms (`treaty.rs`, `loader.rs`): quota share, surplus or excess of loss per life, the panel and its shares, how the reinsurer is paid (its own rate table, or a proportion of the customer's premium), commission, claim notification and consent thresholds, group terms, statement period, profit commission, what a listing may say about a life, and the clauses a disputed item cites. Every treaty ships worked scenarios and they run at load: a treaty that does not do what its file says does not load. Versions are activated through the log and are immutable once active. Cover in force stays on the version it was written under until a recapture or a negotiated move changes it by command.
- **Per-life accumulation.** The module reads every partition's event stream (`feed.rs`), de-duplicates on the sequence and refuses a batch with a gap. Covers are held per life: a life linked to a party accumulates across all policies and partitions; a scheme member not linked to a party accumulates only against their own record. Treaties that name the same `retention_group` share one retention per life.
- **The cession calculation** (`cession.rs`) is a pure function of the covers on one life and their treaty versions. Retention is filled in a fixed order, by start date and then by key, so the answer never depends on the order events arrived in. The result is a timeline per cover. A backdated change is the same calculation with one more date in it, and only what changed is sent on, from the date it changed.
- **Never silently over-retained.** Cover over the retention that no treaty or facultative terms take is recorded as unplaced and is always an open exception. For scheme cover written by a membership file, the excess on a known life is ceded facultatively under the treaty's standing terms for group business, or flagged to group administration for the underwritten-excess path, as the scheme's terms say. A possible match between a member and a party is an exception and is shown to an underwriter, but moves nobody's retention until a person confirms it.
- **The capacity step** (`capacity.rs`) answers, before terms are offered, what is held on a life and whether the treaty takes a new cover automatically. Capacity is reserved for the life of the proposal so that two proposals on one life see each other. Facultative terms are recorded against the reservation and follow it to the issued layer.
- **Ceded premium** (`premium.rs`) is worked out per billing period from the cession timeline and posted as differences, each with the period's own value date. When the core reverses a period's billing in an unwind, the ceded premium for it reverses the same way. A cover that cannot be priced is charged nothing and raised as an exception; a rate is never guessed.
- **Recoveries** (`claims.rs`) follow the cession at the insured event. The expected recovery is posted as recoverable and becomes due from the reinsurer as the claim is paid. Large claims are notified to the panel, and consent is tracked where the treaty gives reinsurers a say. Catastrophe and stop loss covers respond to what the insurer paid and kept across claims.
- **Disputed items.** When an admission is reversed, recoveries already taken are not reversed. Each becomes a disputed item on the reinsurer's account citing the treaty's errors-and-omissions and claims-cooperation clauses, and is settled by two people for nothing, part or all.
- **Statements, listings and settlement** (`account.rs`). A statement takes everything on a reinsurer's account valued up to the period end and nets it. Its listing carries the policy reference, dates, sums and only the facts the treaty allows; every release is logged. A cession that changes for a period already listed flags the statement, blocks its settlement, and is corrected by a supplementary listing that shows each row as sent and as it is now. Settlement needs the reinsurer's agreement where the treaty requires it, a maker and a checker, and no hold; it produces one sweep per partition and one net payment.
- **Reconciliation and comparison** (`views.rs`). `reconcile` rebuilds every cession timeline and checks the accounts against the periods and claims behind them. There are views for exposure by reinsurer, concentration by scheme and site, the migration comparison with legacy retention records, and what a proposed treaty version would change across the in-force book.

## How it talks to the rest

In: `Command::CoreEvents` from the event feed; capacity commands from underwriting and new business; scheme terms and possible matches from group administration; claim facts from claims; statement and settlement commands from operations and month-end.

Out, on each applied command: `Instruction`s for the core (record this cession, post this entry, sweep this balance), each with a stable ID that is its idempotency key there, and `Notice`s for other modules and people (exceptions, underwritten-excess requests, large-claim notifications, consent requests, disputed items, documents to file in write-once Blob Storage, settlements due).

## Known gaps

- **Delivery to the core is not wired yet.** The module produces instructions; the core commands that record a cession on a layer and post reinsurance entries, and the driver that carries instructions to them, are the next piece of work in this module. Until then nothing reinsurance works out reaches the ledger.
- **Hosting.** The state machine is ready for the module host in `ic-raft`, but is not yet registered with the node server, and the feed of partition events into a module group is not on `main` yet.
- **Joint lives.** The design counts joint cover in full against each life's retention. The core has no joint-life cover yet, so there is nothing to count.
- **Longevity reinsurance for annuities** is not built: the core has no annuities.
- **Ceded premium is posted by its own entry**, not as lines in the billing entry as the capacity model in the design notes assumes. The module is the only place that knows the cession, and it runs beside the core, so the posting follows the billing event.
- **Original premium, gross or net.** The core's billing event carries the premium by cover type after discounts. A treaty priced on the original premium before discounts is priced on the same figure until the core sends both.
- **Listings are built in one command.** Cutting a statement walks the whole ceded book and returns the listing as one document. A very large book will need the listing cut by partition.
- **Ceded reserves** are an actuarial figure and are not held here; the exposure view shows balances, open recoverables, disputed items and ceded sums at risk.
- **Stop loss after catastrophe cover** subtracts the recoveries of catastrophe treaties whose period starts inside the stop loss period. A treaty with a different order of cover needs that said in its terms first.
